# Tech Plan

## Platform and delivery

- **Deploys:** see [Deploying](../README.md#deploying).
- **Engine:** Phaser 4 and TypeScript, built with Vite into static files.
- **Hosting:** Cloudflare Workers static assets, no Worker script.
- **Platforms:** web, phone browser first. iOS and Android later by wrapping the web build with Capacitor.
- **Repo:** private GitHub repository `andornaut/dog-walker-game`.
- **Tooling:** follows `andornaut/cloudflare-starter`: Vitest, ESLint with the shared house rules, Prettier, markdownlint, husky and lint-staged, and Dependabot. The Node version, the npm commands and CI are in the [root README](../README.md#developing).

## Site

The pages and `public/` are laid out in the [root README](../README.md#developing). `pages/` is Vite's root, its HTML pages the build inputs, and they load modules as `/src/...`, which `vite.config.ts` aliases to the repository's `src/`. The game imports `vendor/game-kit/src/<path>` as `@andornaut/game-kit/<path>`, mapped in `tsconfig.json`'s `paths` (tsc and tsx) and `vite.config.ts`'s `resolve.alias` (Vite and Vitest). Vite bundles `vite.config.ts` without that alias, so the config loads its plugins from `src/tools/viteConfigModules.ts` through a Vite module runner given it.

## Architecture

The player-facing rules (controls, layout, movers, pulls, feedback) are in `gameplay/design.md`; this section covers how the code carries them out.

### The walk

- **Collisions:** the pack collides as it is drawn. Each part's hit rectangle is the walker's or a dog's opaque pixels, `PART_INSET` inside them, and the pack's outline is their bounding box, leashes included (`src/layout/packFormation.ts`). An obstacle's hit box is the bounding box of its sprites' opaque pixels at its depth, `PART_INSET` inside, cut to its lanes (`obstacleHitBox` in `src/runner/walk.ts`). A standing obstacle's hit box runs up the road no farther than its footprint's rows, `cell.length` each (`hitReach`), so standing art drawn taller than its rows draws over the road behind it but hits only within them; a moving one hits as tall as it is drawn. Pickups and lucky finds use `ROAD_HIT_SIZE` (`src/assets/spriteKeys.ts`). The spawn planner spaces rows by the longest obstacle as it hits (`plannedCellInset` in `src/runner/walk.ts`), so taller standing art never spaces them wider, and measures the pack-fit gap as drawn: each kind at its own hit length (`itemHeight`) and the pack at the tallest pack's hit boxes, its outline (`tallestPackLength` in `src/layout/packFormation.ts`) less `PART_INSET` at each end.
- **Cosmetic layers:** ambient movers (`src/road/ambient.ts`, each zone's mix in `src/data/ambient.ts`), distraction movers (`src/road/distractions.ts`), roadside props (`src/road/roadsideProps.ts`) and hit obstacles left on the road as scenery (`src/road/struckScenery.ts`), and darts that stop on the verge, kept as scenery once the walk drops them off the road (`src/road/vergeScenery.ts`), collide with nothing and the walk never reads them. Ambient movers draw from the run's `ambient` stream and distraction movers from its `distractions` stream, which the session never reads, and roadside props draw no random numbers, so a seed plays the same walk with or without them. Red herring crossers (`src/road/herrings.ts`, from the zone plan's `Road.herrings`, see Movers) draw nothing on the road: `ItemViews` draws each as its mover, in its art at its depth with no cue, from as its row comes on screen until it has crossed, and none with reduced motion. Distraction movers are red herrings: `counts.distractionsPerZone` per zone, one in each equal stretch of it, each gone before its row is down to `multipliers.distractionEndRoadShare` of the road ahead of the pack. They wear the zone's own hazards (`distractionKinds`): its one-lane `onFoot` minor obstacles weighted above 0, by those weights, each only with a walk, hop or run tag; a zone with none takes the ambient walkers. Each takes one of three patterns at `probability.distractionPatternOdds`: it steps from the verge into the edge lane, stands there for `multipliers.distractionVisitLingerShare` of its time on screen and steps back off (visit), waits at the road's edge half on the verge (where a dart waits idle) then leaves (edge), or starts in the edge lane as its row comes into view and stands there for at least `multipliers.distractionLeaveLingerShare` of its time then steps off (leave). A visit walks back off the road no faster than it stepped on, and a test on the shipped tuning holds the linger to that. It shows no move cue: the view draws its body sprite only. None is drawn over a pickup or an obstacle, or stands on a roadside prop's footprint (its drawn width across, from half a cell nearer than its feet to a cell beyond): as its row comes into view, it checks the road's items, the zone's pending spawns and the verge scenery along their paths, and the props' footprints, over its whole time on screen, and takes the side it drew, else the other side, else a row a cell further up its stretch, or is skipped. They sort by row with the roadside props, so a nearer prop stands in front, under every road sprite.
- **Fixed step:** `RunScene` steps the walk session at 1/60 s, as many steps per frame as the wall-clock time since the last frame holds, banking at most a second (`vendor/game-kit/src/clock/fixedStep.ts`). It reads the frame timestamps, not Phaser's delta, which Phaser smooths and clamps, so a slow device keeps to real time and a replay matches the run step for step.
- **Gear drops:** after the rest of a zone's quotas, `spawnQuotas` draws its gear drops from the zone's `spawns` stream (`drawGearDrops` in `src/rules/gearDrops.ts`): `counts.gearDropsPerZone` for the walk position, rounded by `chanceRound` (`vendor/game-kit/src/random.ts`: the whole part, and one more at the fractional part's chance), each item drawn by 1 over its price among the items not in effect (the Pocket's and the found one) and not drawn before it in the zone. A drop's road kind is its gear id (`isGearDrop`), a one-cell quota that blocks no lane, placed like a standing lucky find and drawn as `drop-<gear>`. Touching one grabs it as a lucky find is grabbed, with no hit and no Long reach. The walk holds one found item at a time (`RunState.foundGear`) beside the Pocket's: `grabGear` ends the found item's effects (every modifier, dodge, immunity and skip whose gear source is marked `found`, then the spare treats held cut to the room left (`holdSpareTreats`), then each owner's hearts held to their max), then applies the new item's with a `found` gear source. Each effect acts where its rule reads it, so a found item works mid-walk as the Pocket's does: a maxHearts modifier raises maxes, not hearts. The found item takes the effect well after the Pocket's, and its strip line, popup, hold card and the names a breakdown gives it (`gearSourceName`, `runText.foundGear`) say it was found.
- **Lights:** `src/road/lights.ts` reads the light defs from `shared/lights.json`, which art writes: per key a `shape` (`"ellipse"` or `"band"`, centred on its anchor, or `"cone"`, its apex on its anchor, widening to `width` at `height` away, pointing down), a `width` and `height` in px at full size, a palette `colour`, and 1 to 3 `rings`, outermost first, each a `scale` of the shape (toward the apex for a cone) and a `strength`, each ring inside the last and brighter. `src/scenes/run/lightsView.ts` bakes each def once into a texture whose ring pixels hold their strength of the colour, and draws it with an additive blend over the road, the backdrop's approach and the shadows, under every sprite, scaled with its anchor's depth and faded in from the juncture like the props. A verge's `pools` place lights along its zone's road (`src/road/lightPlaces.ts`): each names a def by `light`, lies `across` from the road's centre line, and repeats `every` road px on both sides from `offset.left` and `offset.right`, counted from the zone's start strip as roadside props' slots are, so lights line up with props; an optional `zones` lists the zone ids it shows in. The table's `attach` lists, by sprite key, the lights every sprite of that key carries, whichever view draws it: drawn last each frame, `LightsView` finds every visible sprite of such a key in the scene, at its feet, scale and flip (`anchorSprite`), and places its lights (`placeAttachedLights`) just under it, at its opacity, cut off at the juncture as it is, while a band mover's stay whole. Every light is culled by its own drawn rect (its texture's at its scale and rotation), once wholly outside the walk view, never by its sprite's bounds or visibility: a roadside prop whose art has left the screen stands hidden as a light carrier (`placeRoadsideLights`), from behind the feet to the cross street, so its lights follow where it stands, while a light whose sprite ends, a mover leaving the road, ends with it. Each names a def by `light` and sits `dx` and `dy` px at full size from the sprite's feet as drawn (whole or half px), or, for a lamp that moves as the sprite animates, by `frames`: an offset for each frame of the sprite's sheet, in sheet order, `null` on a frame that shows no light. It may set a cone's `angle` (degrees as drawn: 0 right, 90 down, 180 left, so a sprite drawn facing the pack shines down at 90), a `pulse` (a strength for each frame of the sprite's sheet, a frame named for its place there, as in `cyclist@75 3.aseprite`), `facesRoad` and `zones`. A flipped sprite mirrors its lights' `dx` and `angle`. A roadside prop is never flipped: a light with `facesRoad` is set out as for a prop on the road's right and mirrored on its left, so a lamp's beam, set from its bulb down and to the left, shines toward the road from either side; any other prop light stays as set. A prop held on its resting frame (see Roadside props) holds its lights at that frame's strength. Validation throws on an unknown field, key, colour or zone, and tests check that each attached sprite exists, that a sheet of several frames has a `pulse` and `frames` entry for each, and that each cone's apex lies on its sprite's lamp, on every frame it shows on, at every depth step, on both sides: on or beside a lit pixel, a white one only where its run of white has a cream ring, as a rider's headlamp has and its chest band, helmet or reflector has not. Lights are drawing only: nothing in the walk, the rules or the simulator reads them.
- **Lucky finds:** each kind's count in a zone is `counts.luckyFindsPerZone` for the kind times the zone's `density.luckyFinds` (`zoneStats`), rounded by `chanceRound` from the zone's `spawns` stream, in the table's order. The zone's finds are then shuffled, and each is a quota of its own, a mover at the zone's mover share where its look moves.
- **Spaced spawns:** a zone's lucky finds, and apart from them its gear drops, are spread so no two stand close: the ith of n takes every other one of 2n - 1 equal stretches of the zone (`spacedStretch` in `src/runner/stretches.ts`, held by `SpawnQuota.within`), so two stand in its first and last thirds. A standing one is placed only within its stretch, and a moving one starts in it or waits for a later row before the stretch ends, else stands still in it.
- **Movers:** `src/runner/moverPlanning.ts` plans each mover's whole path from the zone's spawn stream, and `src/runner/movers.ts` moves it along that path, so a seed replays the same moves. A hopper's ways to hop grow as its hop shapes to the power of its count, so it takes at most `HOP_SEQUENCES_MOST` sequences of each count, drawn per zone from the movers stream where there are more (`hopSequences`). Movement kinds keep their data names, the rulebook's archetypes mapping dash and swoop to `dart`, wander to `step`, stalk to `approach` and cruise to `inLane`. The pattern movers (`bounce`, `follow`, `stopAndGo`, `sway`) are steppers whose sequences `moverSpec` (`src/runner/moverSpecs.ts`) lays out with no draw: a bounce's and a sway's steps alternate a lane each way, a follower's are every sideways sequence of its count (2 to the power of it), and a stop-and-go's are pushes in its lane that each travel `factor - 1` of their length along the road. Each step after the first waits its kind's pause (`pauseSeconds`, or `stopSeconds`); a bounce, a follower and a stop-and-go cue every step, the pause stretched to at least the cue. Their count is drawn among their sequences' lengths (`drawnStepCount`). A `dive` is a dart that always stops in a lane, never turning back or stopping on the verge, crossing at `speed` of a dart's and sliding its last lane at `slide` of that (`isSliding`). A `merge` is a rider that, once its travel down the road is `mergeAt` of the way to the pack's rows (no sooner than its cue can show after it enters, and finishing before it reaches them), moves a lane to either side that stays on the road over `mergeSeconds`, after a cue (the wander mark); the planner places it as a rider over both lanes, one placement per side. A mover's body plays its `cue` tag, where its sprite has one, while its cue shows, a diver its `dive` tag while it slides, each once through, and a merging rider its `merge` tag while it moves across. `planSpawns` (`src/runner/zonePlanner.ts`) places the obstacles that open a card first, then the other movers, then the other spawns, so a card is the last thing skipped. The movers go in `placingOrder`: those that may share the fewest cells first (steppers, hoppers and stalkers, then riders, then darts), the widest first among them, so each placed later, freer to share, fits around those placed before. An obstacle blocks a lane unless it resolves as a pull (`blocksLane` in `src/runner/walkItems.ts`), since a pull obstacle such as a squirrel passes through the pack. A moving lucky find or pull obstacle is a prize: placed after every lane-blocking spawn, it keeps its path clear of the spawns placed after it, but blocks no lane and counts toward no cap beside the lane-blocking movers. A darting one may set off over the lane-blocking spawns' cells, as any dart may. In each row's window no dart sweeps every lane of a stretch of road that the other lane-blocking spawns wall off (`openStretches`), so a pack walled into a stretch by a slow rider or a standing obstacle still has a lane there to dodge the dart in. The planner holds the on-screen mover caps (`counts.maxMovers` and its dart and slow-vehicle limits) and the pack-fit gap along the held path (`packFitsOnPath`) wherever a mover can stand. The path's lane changes are spaced for the slowest pack while Muddy (`slowestDebuffedAgility` in `src/rules/walkers.ts`), since Muddy may come in any neighbourhood and last past its end; rows are spaced for the slowest pack's own lane move. A dart that fits no row as drawn, even set off from higher up the road (`DART_LIFTS`), tries every row again at another tilt of its kind's range and then at another speed (`moverRetries`), nearest its drawn one first, before it is dropped: a crossing that sweeps more lanes in the pack's window than leave `counts.openLanesPerRowMin` open fits no row, and a shallower, steeper, slower or faster one often does. A rider retries the same way at another speed, and a stepper, hopper or stalker at another count of moves: a merging rider too fast to finish its merge before it reaches the pack has no placement, and a slower one does. Of the darts that stop, `probability.dartVergeStopShare` stop on the far verge instead of in a lane, decided by the same outcome draw (`dartOutcome`), so it takes no draw of its own: `DART_VERGE_LANES` past the far edge, clear of every zone's curb props. Off the road it is out of play, so the planner plans it as a dart that crosses: it holds its rows, blocks lanes and stands for the pack-fit gap only while it crosses, and no spot of its rests on the road (`stopsOnVerge`). A treat, a lucky find or another spawn that blocks no lane keeps off every spot where a mover may end a move and stand (`moverRests`): a stopped dart's stop, and a stepper's, hopper's or stalker's last spot however it chooses its moves. A tip (`moversRestOn`) may lie there. A mover holds every row its blocking and path touch, but a standing obstacle placed after it only keeps its hit box off the mover's body (`MoverBody`), the road the mover's hit box covers at some moment on screen; a dart has none, as an obstacle may stand where a dart passes. A stop-and-go across (the brewery dray, the crosstown bus, the patrol buggy, the tourist train) is a vehicle, not a dart: level with its row the whole time it is on screen, stops included, its body is every lane its hit box touches from setting off until it has left the road's bottom, from its row up for its hit length, and it is placed only where that body is clear of the standing spawns placed before it, so it never drives through a standing obstacle. It stands once in one of its kind's `stops`, each the lanes it covers there, counted on the road from 0 at the left (the buggy `[[1, 2], [2, 3]]`, the bus, the dray and the train `[[0, 2], [1, 3], [2, 4]]`): it tries the stop its own stop draw picks first, each as likely, on the rows where that stop leaves the path's held lanes open (`leavesPathOpen`), with every retry, and only then its other stops in turn, in a random order the same draw picks (`stopsToTry`): what is left of the draw past the stop it picked, scaled to the stops left, picks the next, so it takes no draw of its own. It is dropped only where no stop fits. A kind with a `herringShare` (the train, at `probability.touristTrainHerringShare`) plans that share of its movers, by their outcome draw, as red herrings (`herringSpawn`): one of its quota's count, no spawn but `Road.herrings`, scenery the walk never reads, in its stretch's row its start draw picks, from the side its side draw picks. It sets off with no cue as its row comes on screen and crosses the whole road without stopping at its drawn speed, drifting up the road at the tilt that has it gone past the far edge just as it is down to `multipliers.distractionEndRoadShare` of the road ahead, and before its zone ends. It takes no cell, blocks no lane, stands nowhere and counts toward no cap. A dart may set off over cells other spawns hold, and a rider may pass over cells only darts hold (`moverMayTake`), or that another lane-blocking rider holds where their hit boxes keep `pixels.riderFollowGap` of road between them at every moment both are on the road (`ridersMeet`): each one's travel grows linearly while it is on the road, so the road between two in a lane is checked over the moments they share; no other mover takes a cell a dart holds. A tip or a treat (`moversPassOver`) may lie on any other cell a mover passes through; any other spawn that blocks no lane keeps off every cell a mover holds. Each lane-blocking spawn, a standing one's cell or a mover's start lane, is drawn toward the lanes left unblocked longest (`laneBalanceWeight`). A dart shows idle at its edge from as soon as its row comes on screen, or from the road's top where it starts farther up the road, and plays its cue `seconds.moverWarningSeconds` before it sets off; a rider's cue shows from as soon as its row comes on screen. The walk's first zone's rows reach back onto the road in view where they fit, the first meeting the pack no sooner than `seconds.firstZoneContact` in, on the zone's row grid; the first lane-blocking standing spawn that fits there takes the first row, before later spawns set it aside for the pack to steer across. A row counts as filled when a lane-blocking obstacle is in view in it as it comes on screen: a standing one, or a mover once its body shows. A fixed obstacle (a minor obstacle that never moves, `fixedKinds`) with no room in its stretch or anywhere else in the zone is swapped for one of the zone's narrower fixed obstacles, a lane narrower at a time down to one, drawn by their weights among those that fit, so the road keeps its count of obstacles; a card obstacle, a puddle, a pothole or a mover standing still is never swapped. After the lane-blocking spawns, a run of more than `counts.maxEmptyRows` unfilled rows gets one of the zone's own fixed obstacles, a skipped one or one moved from the busiest stretch, under every placement rule; a run none can take, such as one held clear for a mover's path, stays.
- **Pausing:** the walk steps only while `walkPaused` (`src/hud/topBar.ts`) finds nothing pausing it. Each overlay reports to `MenuFlow.pause()` (`src/scenes/run/menuFlow.ts`), so a new overlay pauses the walk too.
- **Pseudo-3D road:** game logic keeps a flat road (lane x, distance ahead of the walker in road pixels, scale 1 at the pack). Only drawing projects it, through `src/layout/projection.ts` with `ROAD_CAMERA` (`src/data/screen.ts`). `src/layout/roadRaster.ts` draws the road and verges row by row into one canvas texture, up to the backdrop's cross street: on the band's junction rows (`junctionRows`, between the near corners) it draws the road surface alone, and on its approach rows (`roadIntoBand`) the verge too wherever the backdrop shows the verge's ground, seams, dots or marks, so the backdrop's own still detail never shows beside the moving verge. A row covering several strip rows keeps the strip's lines along the road (runs of 8 or more rows down a column) wherever it covers them, and its seams across the road (runs of 8 or more columns, under 8 rows thick, such as paving joints) on exactly one run of rows per pass, so no seam blinks however far away it is. Road sprites scale smoothly with distance, drawn from the smallest of their art steps (1x and the manifest's variants at every 1/16 down to half size, `<key>@94` to `@50` in `shared/depth-steps.json`, that are loaded) at least their projected size (`src/layout/depthScale.ts`), shrunk by at most 1/16 of full size with linear filtering, so 1 px details fade rather than blink as they approach.
- **Pulls:** `src/runner/pulls.ts` draws no random numbers: each pull follows from the road, so the input log needs nothing for them. A darting squirrel is a target from its cue on, never while it waits idle at the road's edge (`isWaiting`); the scene and the simulator both take their pulls from `stepPulls`, so the one check covers both. A pull into a lane where a lane-blocking obstacle or mover would reach the pack before the player could steer back (`seconds.pullReactionWindow`) waits until the lane clears. A squirrel blocks no lane and its path may cross obstacles' cells, so this check at the moment of the pull, not the planner, keeps every pull fair.
- **Roadside props:** `src/road/roadsideProps.ts` places them from `shared/roadside.json`, per zone by art id, on a grid of slots `slot` road px apart along each side. Lawn props stand beyond the sidewalk, each side alternating lots and gaps. Curb props (a zone's optional `curb`: `groups` of props, and per side a `start` and `stride`) take a group at every slot and stand wholly inside the zone's curb band (`curbBand` in `src/road/roadside.ts`): the sidewalk, 2 road px in from each edge, paving counting as a sidewalk 44 px wide (`sidewalkTo`); a verge with no sidewalk has no band. Verge movers stop 32 road px past the sidewalk (`vergeStop` in `src/road/ambient.ts`), so a stopped mover's frame stays clear of the band; sidewalk walkers pass in front of curb props. No prop stands on a zone's start strip. Lawn and curb props share one pool of `ROADSIDE_LIMIT` sprites, drawn through the projection from their depth steps like road sprites. Props stand up to the cross street (the stage's `reach`, the junction's top row), past the road's far edge, and fade in with their shadows from there to the far edge (`fadeIn` in `src/road/roadClip.ts`); movers and items come into view at the far edge, opaque. A road sprite standing on the road draws whole, its top over the backdrop band; one standing past the cross street shows nothing above it. A prop whose sprite has an `idle` tag of two or more frames loops it, each frame shown for its own duration from the sheet, on the walk's ambient clock, which holds while the walk is paused or a firing holds it; a one-frame tag, or none, draws still. Each placed prop starts its loop at its own phase, a share of the loop's length hashed from its road distance from the walk's start and its signed across (`roadsidePhase`), so props of one kind never change in step and a prop keeps its phase as it scrolls. Every depth step shows the same frame, so each step's sheet carries the same frames and durations under the tag. With reduced motion a prop holds the tag's first frame, so that frame is its resting look.
- **Spare treats:** a `spareTreats` modifier, such as the Fanny pack's, sets how many spare treats the pack holds (`spareTreatRoom` in `src/rules/spareTreats.ts`), counted in `RunState.spareTreats`. A treat grabbed while every walking owner is at their max is held when there is room (`storeSpareTreat`), and otherwise pays `money.treatCash` (`grabTreat` in `src/rules/treats.ts`): a money change whose base cause is the treat, carried cash like a tip's, but never a tip, so no tip modifier, tip label or tip tally touches it. The walk step reports it as its treat's `cash`, and the scene and the simulator both take it from the walk session. After changes made at once (a step's bumps in `stepWalk`, or the effects of a card, a fork, a contact or a lucky find in `outcomeOf`), `spendSpareTreat` gives `hearts.treat` back to the owner they lowered who is left with the fewest hearts, ties to the front dog, before `fireOwnersAtZero`. A loss Second chance forgave whole lowers no meter, so it spends none. The walk step and the outcome report it as `spareTreat`, and the scene and the simulator both take it from the walk session.
- **Verges:** `src/road/roadside.ts` reads each zone's verge from `shared/verges.json`, by art id: outward from the road's edge, the curb to `curbTo`, the strips, then the sidewalk or paving to `walkTo` (null: paved to the screen's edges) and the lawn. Every place across is in road px from the road's centre line (the road's edge is 160), and every length along in road px of walk distance, so seams, dots and marks scroll with the road. A verge's optional `pools` are lights repeating along it (see Lights).
- **Walk session:** `src/runner/session.ts` is the walk's one step order, pure and shared by `RunScene` and the simulator. A step applies its inputs (lane moves, heading home), then the pulls, the Zoomies' drift and the walk, then any firing hold; then it opens the queued cards, ends the zone (fees, then the fork) or plans the next zone. Its random streams are named from the run's seed (`runStreams`, see Random streams), so a seed and an input log replay the same walk wherever they are played. It returns events that the scene plays as sounds and effects and the simulator counts.

### Screens and UI

- **Accessibility:** reduced motion (a setting, defaulting to `prefers-reduced-motion`) drops decorative motion but keeps every highlight and state change, the road scroll and the lane slide. No state relies on colour alone. Tap targets are at least 44 game pixels. Text meets 4.5:1 contrast and essential icons 3:1.
- **Badge cards:** the Logbook's Badges tab and the end screen open one card for a badge (`addBadgeDetail` in `src/scenes/badges.ts`: the badge at 2x with its name, status, goal and line, over the screen dimmed, the Logbook's under its top bar; a locked badge's status, Locked, in `GREYED.shortPrice`, the dark red of a shop price the bank does not cover), so a badge reads alike on both. The end screen's earned badges have no tap action of their own, so a tap and a press and hold on one both open its card: the hold opens it as the hold time passes, with `TILE_SLOP_PX`, and its release taps nothing. The strip holds 7 badges to a row (`BADGE_STRIP` in `src/home/badgesView.ts`); past 7 it takes two rows, split as evenly as they go (8 as 4+4, 9 as 5+4), each slot the fuller row's share of the width and each row centred, as a celebration's does too. The end screen's two-row strip grows 8px up into the panel's room, its bottom kept over Head home (`endBadgeStrip` in `src/home/endLayout.ts`). The badges' second row comes before the owners' second lines (`grantedQuipLines`), and stays one row only where the panel with every closing line on one line would not end above two (`endBadgeRows`): 6 breakdown lines and 3 owners' rows, which takes a Start cash line, a dev start's. Each badge's tap target is its slot (`badgeStripHits`): in one row, from the badges' top down to the panel's frame; in two, the first row's 44 tall ending at its badges' bottom and the second's from there down to the frame. Every target is at least 44 each way up to 14 badges; past 14 a row holds 8 or more and its targets narrow under 44. With a card open, any tap closes it, the close control and the page beside the game included, as do Escape, Enter and back before they take Head home (`endTargetAt` in `src/home/endView.ts`).
- **Change labels:** each change shows a label with its amount alone ("−1♥", "+$2", a Stinky tip's "+$0"; `changeLabels` in `src/hud/hudView.ts`); the message strip says what caused it. Each label draws on a cream chip, a gain in forest and a loss or a zeroed "+$0" in dark red (`changeLabelColour` in `src/scenes/changeLabelColours.ts`). A heart change pops over its dog and flies into its card (`src/scenes/heartFeedback.ts`), its label fading on the card's bottom rows (`hudLanes`). A money gain's label, a tip's (a Stinky "+$0" one included), a treat's cash, a lucky find's or a card option's, pops by the cash total, wherever the money changed hands: `HudScene` hangs it under the walk bar, centred under the cash well, the first slot `MONEY_LABEL.gap` rows under the bar and each later one stacked under the one before, drawn over the message strip and the debuff bars (`HUD_DEPTH.cashChip`), in the first slot no label showing there holds (`cashChipPlace`, `freeCashSlot`). ### Source layout

Each context keeps its pure code at its root and the code that imports Phaser in a `phaser/` subfolder.

- `vendor/game-kit/`: helpers with no game knowledge, licensed MIT ([README](../vendor/game-kit/README.md)). A copy of the game-kit repository's `kit/`, written by its `scripts/sync-into.sh` with the commit in `KIT_COMMIT`; never edited here, and linted there rather than here. A kit change lands as a game-kit commit, then a sync commit here
- `src/assets/`: manifest and Aseprite parsing, art keys, depth steps, the atlas manifest and atlas groups
- `src/audio/`: sound cues, the zones' music and its fades, the music and effects mix, and the sound format
- `src/crash/`: the crash screen
- `src/data/`: content and tuning
- `src/dataPage/`: the `/data/` page, with filter pills kept in the query string
- `src/homePage/`: the home page's Install button
- `src/headers/`: the build-time security headers
- `src/home/`, `src/help/`, `src/hud/`: view models for the home, settings, stats and end screens, the help screen, and the walk bar and pause menu
- `src/input/`: keys and the touch gesture state machine
- `src/layout/`: screen geometry and the road projection
- `src/pack/`: the pack drawing every screen shares: each leash from the walker's hand to the dog's collar and the draw order (dogs by their feet rows, then leashes, then the walker). `phaser/` draws the pack for the walk, the start screen's gate and the end screen's walk home, so the three draw it alike
- `src/pwa/`, `src/dev/`: phone app and dev tools
- `src/road/`: road clipping, the roadside, ambient and distraction movers, and struck and verge scenery
- `src/rules/`, `src/state/`: run state and pure rules
- `src/runner/`: the walk session, lane motion, spawn planning, collisions, movers and pulls. `walk.ts` steps the walk over the road items `walkItems.ts` sorts into kinds; `zonePlan.ts` turns a zone's data into spawn quotas, which `zonePlanner.ts` places on the row grid and path of `spawning.ts`, keeping its cells in `spawnCells.ts`; `moverSpecs.ts` reads each mover's spec from the data, `moverPlanning.ts` draws and places its path, and `movers.ts` moves it along that path
- `src/save/`: persistence
- `src/scenes/`, `src/main.ts`: the Phaser scenes
- `src/sim/`: the run simulator
- `src/site/`: tests of the site nav's stylesheet, `public/nav.css`
- `src/testing/`: helpers shared by the tests
- `src/tools/`: the art manifest, the data export and the depth preview; `atlas/` packs the atlases and hashes the art at build time
- `src/ui/`: the pixel-art UI kit; `phaser/` draws text, panels, the dimmed ramps and the grey copies of disabled art
- `src/units.ts`: the game's branded units, money and hearts

**Boundary:** only `src/main.ts`, `src/scenes/` and the `phaser/` folders may import Phaser or code that does, and only tests may import `src/testing/`. A context listed in `CONTEXTS` (`eslint.config.mjs`) imports only the folders and modules its row lists. No value imports form a cycle (`import-x/no-cycle`); type-only imports are erased, so they may. ESLint enforces all four (the game-kit repository's lint holds the kit to importing nothing from a game), so all game logic runs in unit tests without a renderer.

### Assets

- **Atlases:** a build packs every image and sprite frame into atlas pages of at most 2048 px a side (`src/tools/atlas/atlasPlugin.ts`), grouped `boot`, `ui`, `home`, `run` (the choice cards included) and `road`, with an atlas manifest mapping each key and frame to its page and rect. `road` holds the road art drawn between depth steps: every depth step variant and the 1x of each sprite that has one, except the `home` group's 1x art (each dog, owner and walker, the tip coin and the home screen's effects, `isHomeArt` in `src/assets/atlasGroups.ts`). WebGL sets a texture's filter for the whole page, so `road` pages alone filter linearly (`smoothDepthArt` in `src/assets/phaser/manifestArt.ts`, which filters the same keys' own textures on the dev server); every other page stays nearest, and the canvas's integer zoom stays crisp, since filtering applies only inside the game's own resolution. `home`, `run` and `road` sprite frames are trimmed to their opaque pixels with their offsets recorded, so origins, flips and anchors measure the whole frame. BootScene gives each key its own texture whose frames are rects of the shared page, so keys, frame names and `<texture>:<tag>` animations work unchanged. It loads `boot`, then `ui` and `home` and the home screen's sounds (`HOME_CUES`), shows the home screen, and loads the rest behind it; Start walk and Help wait for those. `src/scenes/homeArt.test.ts` fails for an art key or sound the home screen names that boot leaves until later. The dev server loads every key from its own files.
- **Checks:** the art tests fail when the committed manifest differs from what the files give, and a test fails when `shared/export/` is stale.
- **Data export:** `shared/export/game-data.json` holds the world, content, card faces, tuning and help pages, with their art keys, and `shared/export/layout.json` the screen regions, lanes, HUD, camera, depth steps, sprite sizes, pack formations and scene panels, for art's tools. A build writes them first and the dev server rewrites them as `src/` changes.
- **Depth preview:** `src/tools/depthPreviewCli.ts` draws a road sprite through the walk's projection, depth steps and road raster at a sweep of distances on each zone's road, writing a PNG per zone and distance to `out/depth-preview/<key>/<zone art>/` and a contact sheet to `out/depth-preview/<key>/`.
- **Hashed art:** a build writes every art file the game loads (the art and atlas manifests, the atlas pages, the fonts, the sounds and any key the atlas leaves out) under `/assets/art/`, with a content hash in its name (`src/tools/atlas/hashedArt.ts`). The map from each file's path under `/art/` to its hashed name is compiled into the game's code as the `virtual:art-files` module, which `artUrl` (`src/assets/phaser/manifestArt.ts`) reads, so each version's code loads only its own build's art. The dev server's map is empty, so it loads every file at its own path. `public/art/` is copied as it is too, for the art gallery and the site's pages.
- **Keys:** kebab-case and unique. Dog-keyed art (`dog-<art>`, `face-dog-<art>`, `portrait-dog-<art>`, `owner-<art>` and the like) and zone-keyed art (`backdrop-<art>`, `road-<art>` and the like) name their dog or zone by the `art` id in its data, so a renamed dog or zone keeps its art. Animation keys are `<texture>:<tag>`. Sprites anchor on the feet, and a missing key draws a placeholder box.
- **Manifest:** `public/art/manifest.json` is derived from the files under `public/art/` and committed (`src/tools/artManifest.ts`): a key is its file's name less the extension, and its kind is its folder (`sprites/`, `images/`, `fonts/`, `sounds/`). `public/art/manifest.extra.json` holds what a file cannot say, such as a sound's `loop`. A build writes it before packing the atlases, and the dev server rewrites it as the art changes. Valibot validates it and the sprite JSON at boot.
- **Shared tables:** the files in `shared/` are `depth-steps.json` (each depth step's key suffix and scale, and the frame size rule), `lights.json` (the light defs, which art writes), and the tables art's tools generate, `palette.json` (`art/tools/pixel.py palette`), `verges.json` (`art/tools/backdrops.py verges --write`) and `roadside.json` (`art/tools/roadside.py table --write`, which a test compares against). Never edit a generated table by hand.

### Tests and simulator

- **Simulator:** `src/sim/` plays whole runs on the real rules over a matrix of card policies, route policies, skill presets (`src/sim/skills.ts`), gear, packs and walkers. A player is a steering bot that plays the game's own walk session at its 1/60 s step: its lane moves are the steps' inputs and the policies choose each card as it opens, so a run's seed and its input log (`RunResult.inputs`) replay it in the game. The bot and the policies draw from streams of their own (`bot`, `policy`), so results are deterministic per seed. The bot reads movers by sight (`src/sim/sight.ts`), only what a player sees: a dart's cue, then its own tilt and speed once it sets off, and its turn back or stop once it happens; a rider's speed once it enters; a step once its wander mark shows, as every shape its kind may take there (a bounce's or a sway's way back once it has stepped aside, a stop-and-go's push), and an uncued step once it starts; a dive's stop once it lands and slides; a merge's way once its cue shows. Until then it reads a mover at its kind's speed, across its kind's tilt range and as standing still, and it replans its reaction time after a mover shows something new, so hits from what a mover hid measure how unpredictable it is. It reads two debuffs late, by its preset's `driftReactionSeconds`: it reacts to a Zoomies drift that long after the drift, planning until then from the lane control the pack would have without it (the walk's lane changes, pulls and steps applied to it), and it plans with the pack's lane speed before a Muddy until that long after the Muddy begins, so both cost bumps as they would a player. `--bot foresight` reads every mover's whole planned path instead, every way a stalker or shy hopper may choose its moves included, to check the planner keeps the road passable. The tuning counts are aims: each zone's plan (`Road.planned`, the quota counts from the tuning counts and weights, beside `Road.pending`, the spawns placed) is counted by walk position and zone (`src/sim/delivery.ts`), and the delivery table gives placed/planned per plan for tips, treats, lucky finds, gear drops, minor obstacles standing, darting, riding and hopping or stalking, and obstacles that open a card or not; `--delivery kinds` adds a line per kind, and `--out` writes the sums in each row's `delivery`. Each `--out` row also gives the badge counters per walk (`BadgeCounters` in `src/sim/report.ts`), judged by the achievements' own conditions on each run's facts: `meanTallies` (every lifetime tally), `meanOptionPicks` (each card option's picks, by encounter and option id), `meanBumpsByKind` (landed bumps by minor obstacle), `debuffsGot` (each debuff's `mean` and `p90` times in one walk), and the shares of walks with `cleanZoneShare` (a clean zone), `allCleanShare` (every zone clean), `lastHeartShare` (finished with an owner on their last heart) and `fullPackBonusShare` (finished with every dog of a full pack home; null for a smaller pack). Each row's `meanEarnings` and `p10` to `p90` count what walks banked, and `meanCashLost` the cash per walk lost by ending early (see Earnings). Each row's `meanSpareTreatsStored` and `meanSpareTreatsSpent` count the spare treats a walk held and gave back (`RunState.spareTreats`). Each row's `debuffCosts` gives each debuff's `meanBumpsWhile` (landed bumps while it lasted) and `meanCentsLost` (the cash it took, as the walk's changes name it: its share of each tip's pay, such as Stinky's, and the cash bumps cost while it made them cost cash, such as Spotlight's), per walk; a bump in the step a debuff starts in counts toward none. Each row's `debuffsByZone` splits a walk by zone id, per walk: `clear` gives the `meanBumps` and `meanSeconds` walked with no debuff on the pack, and `debuffs` gives each debuff's `meanBumpsWhile`, `meanPeriods` (the times the pack got it there) and `meanSeconds` (the walking time it lasted there), so a debuff's bumps per period compare against the zone's bump rate without one. The card policies (`src/sim/policies.ts`) call an option safe when it leaves no owner whose meter falls at `LOW_HEARTS` or less, and the free option the one that keeps the most money, then the most hearts, so a policy taking a free option takes a debuff option over one that costs hearts. `debuffs` takes a safe debuff option whenever one is offered, else plays as `mixed`, and `noDebuffs` plays as `mixed` among the options that give no debuff while one of them is safe, else among them all. Each row's `policyFallbackShare` is the share of cards chosen on which `debuffs` (no safe debuff option) or `noDebuffs` (no safe option that gives no debuff) fell back to play as `mixed`, and null for the other policies. Its `--help` lists the options and columns. Compare balance changes before and after at the same seed.
- **Tests:** Vitest over all pure logic; scenes are checked by playing and by headless screenshots.

## Game data

- **Card text:** generated from the effects and tuning, and exported with every card in `shared/export/game-data.json`.
- **Bump costs:** a plain bump costs its dog's owner hearts (`bumpParts` in `src/rules/run.ts`), and, while a debuff with `bumpMoney` such as Spotlight lasts and there is cash, that cash too (`bumpCash`), all of it when there is less; `bumpMoney` modifiers change it. Forgiving modifiers, such as Second chance's, forgive hearts only.
- **Choices:** `cardFor` builds a card (text, affected dogs, options disabled when unaffordable); `chooseOption` applies a choice and returns the new run state plus the outcome events the scenes animate.
- **Earnings:** the walk carries one `money` total: tips, a treat's cash, lucky finds and card options change it, and each zone's end pays every walking dog into it and records the total as `RunState.zoneEndMoney` (the starting money before any zone's end). `walkEarnings` (`src/rules/run.ts`) sets what a walk banks by how it ends (`endReasonOf`, heading home for a walk not yet ended): a finished walk banks all its money; one that ends early, heading home, interrupted and banked from its checkpoint on the next launch, or with every owner having fired you, banks `zoneEndMoney`, or all its money when card costs have left less, and loses the rest of the cash it carried (`RunResult.lostCents`). The pause menu's note says what Head home keeps, and the Home question what it gives up: that loss plus the current zone's pay (`src/hud/homeQuestion.ts`). The end screen's breakdown counts each money change under its base cause (`earningsKind` in `src/home/endView.ts`), a treat's cash in the Tips row, and shows the loss as a Cash lost line, and the total, the best walks, lifetime earnings and the money badges all count only what was banked. The simulator banks by the same rule.
- **Effects:** card options are lists of effects from a closed set of kinds, naming money amounts and modifiers by their `tuning.ts` key. Hearts deltas are written inline, each to a target: the affected dogs, the whole pack, or the lead dog (the front affected dog in formation order). Any card option may apply any debuff. A new kind is code in `src/rules/` plus a test.
- **Format:** typed TypeScript modules in `src/data/`. Content is `as const satisfies Type`. Tuning uses plain `satisfies` so its values stay `number` for live tuning.
- **Integrity:** `src/data/data.test.ts` checks the rulebook's invariants, and type-level tests show invalid data fails to compile.
- **Live tuning:** editing a value file under the dev server updates the running game through Vite HMR. Scenes and rules read tuning at the time of use.
- **Ownership:** gameplay edits values and content in the value files. Tech owns `types.ts`, `index.ts`, `screen.ts`, `src/rules/` and the tests.
- **Units:** money (integer cents), hearts, seconds, probability, pixels and multipliers are branded types (`Unit` in `vendor/game-kit/src/units.ts`, money and hearts in `src/units.ts`), which `read` and `readTable` (`src/rules/units.ts`) give for tuning values; value files hold plain numbers.
- **Neighbourhood-only kinds:** an obstacle or minor obstacle with `zones` appears only in those zones: `src/data/data.test.ts` requires its weight above 0 in each and 0 in every other zone. `zones` holds strings, since `ZoneId` comes from the world, whose types name these types.
- **Zones and forks:** `world.ts` lists the zones from easiest to hardest. A walk is `walkLength` zones from `firstZone`, ending on `lastZone`. The run's `route` lists the zones walked by id, and `zoneIndex` is a position in it. After each zone but the last, `offerFork` offers up to three unwalked zones in world order, each carrying an upgrade; the fork before the last zone leads only to `lastZone`. Its choice is an ordinary card choice that takes the upgrade and appends the zone to the route.

## Save data

- **Achievements:** `src/rules/achievements.ts` judges each badge's conditions when a run is banked, on a purchase and on launch. The counters that span walks live in the stats: lifetime tallies of what happens on the road and of lucky finds (`dodges` counts every dog's dodge, a dodge effect's included), landed bumps by kind, every card option pick by encounter and option id (`optionPicks`, which no tally duplicates; an option's `id` in `encounters.ts` is its save key, so keep it across renames), `noFiringStreak`, the finished walks in a row with no firing: a walk with a firing resets it to 0, and a walk headed home early with no firing leaves it unchanged, and `runs`, the walks by how they ended: `walksPlayed` counts every one, and `walksFinished` only `runs.finished`, the walks that finished every zone from the first. A run keeps its own share of the tallies, bumps and picks in its counters, checkpoint included, until it is banked.
- **Contents:** bank, owned dogs, gear, slots and walkers, chosen walker, last loadout, stats and logbook, shown hints, whether the help has been seen, settings, earned achievements and the checkpoint (with the run's route), all referenced by data ids.
- **Derived values:** the save holds nothing a load can rebuild from the rest of it or the game data, and nothing the game never reads. The total times fired is each dog's added up, and the best walk's earnings are the largest best by pack size (`src/save/stats.ts`). Earned achievements are ids, with no time. `owned` leaves out the starting dog slots and the free dogs and walkers, which every load adds back (`storedSave` in `src/save/schema.ts`). The last walk's firings are dog ids. A checkpoint's dogs keep only the zone each owner fired you in, and it keeps no count of owners on their last heart, which only a finished walk uses. Lifetime bumps by kind and option picks count every kind and option, not only those a badge reads, and the settings are stored in full, defaults included.
- **Format:** one JSON document under `dog-walker-game:save` with a `version` field; every write replaces it.
- **Scenes:** `src/scenes/profileStore.ts` holds the loaded save for every scene and is the only way scenes read or change it.
- **Scope:** one profile per browser, local only, per origin.
- **Storage:** the game kit's save store (`@andornaut/game-kit/save/*`) on an async interface, with the game's keys and its format (`gameSaveStore` in `src/save/storage.ts`, `saveFormat` in `src/save/load.ts`). The web adapter wraps `localStorage`; a Capacitor `Preferences` adapter will replace it in the app, where iOS can evict web storage. When storage fails, the game stays playable and says progress won't be saved.
- **Validation:** a Valibot schema parses every load. Unknown ids are dropped. A save that fails validation is kept under `dog-walker-game:save-backup` and the game starts fresh.
- **Version:** before v1 ships, a save-format change or an id rename (a dog, gear item, walker or encounter) bumps `SAVE_VERSION` (`src/save/schema.ts`) and an older save resets to a default save, kept as a backup like one that fails validation; there are no migrations. A save from a later version than the running game (after a rollback, or on a stale page) is left as stored: the game plays from a default save in memory, writes nothing for the session, and the home screen shows the update plate in its newer-save mode (below).
- **Writes:** on a purchase, a loadout, walker, settings or hint change, the help's first close, after each zone and as the tab hides mid-walk (the checkpoint), and at run end. An unfinished run is banked on the next launch as a run that headed home.

## Phone

- **Audio:** resumes from iOS's `interrupted` or `suspended` state on each return to the foreground and each gesture (`touchend` on iOS), unless the settings mute it.
- **Back:** `guardBackNavigation` (`src/pwa/backNavigation.ts`) catches back two ways, in every browser but Firefox for Android's installed app (`isFirefoxAndroidApp`: that browser, with a display-mode query for fullscreen, standalone or minimal-ui matching). That app ignores a script's close, and its back with nothing to go back to shows a blank screen before it closes, which the page cannot skip, so `setupPhoneApp` leaves back there to the browser: no close watcher, no guard entry and no Quit?. Where the browser has `CloseWatcher` (Chrome 120 and later, Firefox 149 and later), a close watcher, created at load and again after each `close` it fires, takes the Android back gesture or button; it adds no history entry, so a launch with nothing before the game keeps one entry, which lets `window.close()` close the window. A close caused by an Escape keydown is not a back press, since the game's own keys handle Escape. Firefox for Android hands back to a close watcher only when its history can go back, and otherwise leaves the page, so `backCloseWatcher` uses none in a Firefox for Android tab. Where there is no close watcher, or the history already holds more than one entry (a page before the game), a guard history entry takes back by being popped, pushed again on each pop; it also catches a desktop browser's Back button, which is not a close request. The user's back skips an entry with no user interaction recorded on it (Firefox) or one that added an entry without user activation (Chrome), and with nothing before it is the browser's own. So the guard is pushed only from input the browser records before the page sees it (`BACK_GUARD_GESTURES`: a keydown other than Escape, or a pointerup; a touch's pointerdown is recorded on no entry), never at load, and back before the player's first input leaves the game. Each back, in a tab or installed, dispatches `PHONE_EVENTS.backPressed`, which the running scene on top takes (`onBackPressed`): an overlay closes (`listenDismiss`), the walk acts as `walkBackAction` (`src/hud/topBar.ts`) says, the end screen goes home, and the start screen closes its detail card or, with none, opens `QuitScene` (`homeBackAction`). Quit? is drawn by `addQuestionPanel` (`src/scenes/questionPanel.ts`), as the pause menu and the Home question are, and its Quit calls `leaveGame`, which drops the close watcher. With one history entry (an installed app's launch, or a tab opened on the game) it calls `window.close()`. Otherwise it goes back over the guard without pushing it again, then back past the game's own entry, and calls `window.close()` if the page is still there after `LEAVE_WAIT_MS`. Where the browser refuses the close, nothing catches the next back (an installed app closes) unless the player's input first restores the watcher or the guard.
- **Install offer:** `/` and `/play/` both link the manifest, whose `scope` is `/` and `start_url` `/play/`, so an install from either opens the game. `watchInstall` (`src/pwa/installFlow.ts`) holds Chromium's `beforeinstallprompt`, its default prevented so the browser shows no banner of its own, and follows `appinstalled` and the display mode; `installState` (`src/pwa/installState.ts`) reports `installed`, `prompt`, `menuSteps` or `unsupported`. `menuSteps` is a browser that never fires `beforeinstallprompt` and installs only from its own menu, found from the user agent by `menuInstallBrowser`: iOS (any iOS browser, or an iPad asking for the desktop site) and Firefox for Android (`Android` and `Firefox/`, with no `Chrom`). Firefox opens the installed page in `standalone` display mode, so the display-mode query finds it installed. While it is `prompt` or `menuSteps`, Settings has an Install row (its label, a note and a Get button, the whole row one tap target) and the home page an Install button beside Play (`src/homePage/main.ts`, its words in `pages/index.html`). A tap calls the held prompt's `prompt()` before any `await`, while the tap still counts as a gesture; a prompt shows once, so after the player's choice the offer hides until the browser hands over a new one on a later visit. In `menuSteps` the tap opens that browser's steps (`menuText.settings.installSteps.<browser>`): a card over Settings, led by the glyph of the browser's button (`icon-share` on iOS, `icon-browser-menu` in Firefox, each left out while missing), and a `<dialog>` on the home page, which shows the `data-browser` section of `pages/index.html` for the browser, its words checked against the data.
- **Install suggestion:** after the first finished walk, the walker's bubble on the start screen says `homeText.bubble.install` once per device, in place of the how-to or welcome back, while the game offers installing (`walkerLineKey`). The flag `dog-walker-game:install-nudged` (`src/pwa/installNudge.ts`) sits in the save's storage under its own key, not in the save, since installing is per device; while it is unread or unreadable the suggestion counts as shown.
- **Installable web app:** `/manifest.webmanifest` and a service worker, `/sw.js`, built by `src/pwa/precachePlugin.ts`, precache the game and its hashed art (sounds in one format only). HTML, the unhashed art manifest, `/manifest.webmanifest` and the site nav are network-first; hashed assets (the hashed art included), precached files and sounds are cache-first. `updateAction` (`src/pwa/updateReady.ts`) decides updates: a fresh load holds the boot for up to 4 seconds while it checks for a new version; before the player interacts, a waiting version takes over at once and reloads; after, the page never reloads on its own and the home screen shows the update plate, drawn in the canvas under the top bar in place of the walker's bubble (`HOME_LAYOUT.updatePlate`), until its Update now button is tapped to take the new version and reload. `src/pwa` tells `StartScene` through `PHONE_EVENTS.updatePlate` and `updatePlateShown`. The same plate shows, in `homeText.newerSave`'s words, while the save on this device is from a newer version and no update waits (`gateTop`: a waiting update wins, so one plate shows); its button (`acceptUpdate`) takes a waiting version if one has arrived, or else reloads the page so the browser fetches the newer version. It checks again on each arrival home after input, and each time the page comes back into view on the home screen (`resumeCheckDue`: online, at most once a minute), since an installed app usually resumes without relaunching. Mid-walk or in a menu, a resume does nothing.
- **Installed app:** on `/play/` in `fullscreen` or `standalone` display mode, or with iOS's `navigator.standalone`, `public/nav.js` leaves the nav bar out, so no link leaves the app. The game makes the same test once, on first use (`devToolsOn` in `src/dev/devTools.ts`, from `pageRunsAsApp`), and the installed app plays as shipped: it mounts no dev panel (see Dev panel), so `#game` takes the bottom safe-area inset, sets no cheats, applies no queued save edits, plays no replay log and reads no run setup from its URL (`dev=1` included).
- **Persistent storage:** at boot of the installed app (`installState` reads `installed`), `requestPersistentStorage` (`src/pwa/storagePersistence.ts`) asks `navigator.storage.persist()` once to keep the site's storage, the save included, from eviction under storage pressure or after Safari's 7 days without a visit, unless `persisted()` says it is kept already. A browser tab never asks, so Firefox never prompts the player; it only reads `persisted()`. Boot never waits for it, and a missing API, a refusal or an error leaves storage as it was. The dev panel's sheet shows the answer.
- **Phone events** (`PHONE_EVENTS` in `src/pwa/events.ts`): going back (in a browser tab), hiding the app and turning sideways each open the pause menu, as does a window `blur` (`RunScene`); hiding also writes the checkpoint. The run holds a screen wake lock.
- **Portrait only:** the manifest locks `portrait`, and a phone on its side gets a "Turn your phone upright" notice over the page.
- **Security headers:** `src/headers/securityHeadersPlugin.ts` writes `dist/_headers` at build time: nosniff, referrer and permissions policies, and a CSP per path allowing `'self'` plus hashes of each page's inline blocks. No script injects a `<style>`, so no hash depends on a script's text. Content-hashed output under `/assets/*`, the hashed art included, is cached immutably, and the build fails for an unhashed name there. Font descriptors (`*.fnt`) are served as `text/xml`, which Cloudflare compresses.
- **Sound format:** each sound ships as Ogg and AAC (`.m4a`). The page picks the first the browser plays (`src/audio/soundFormat.ts`), boot loads only that file, and the page registers `/sw.js?sound=<format>` because a worker has no `canPlayType`.
- **Touch:** the canvas takes no browser gestures (`touch-action: none`). A tap's `fx-tap` burst plays in its own scene over every other but the dev overlay (`TapBurstScene`), started with the game and never paused or stopped, so it plays out over whatever the tapped scene does next. A page, overlay, card or confirm that a tap opens opens at once, the burst playing on over it. A button that dips (`FEEDBACK.pressDipMs`) acts once its dip has played, except one that opens a page or card, which acts as the finger lifts, its dip playing on under what it opened (`opens` in `src/input/targetPress.ts`, `afterMsUnderOverlay` in `src/scenes/overlayClock.ts`). A card or confirm drawn in its own scene opens as the tap acts; an overlay scene, and a top-bar page, opens at Phaser's next step, which applies queued scene operations, so a second open asked of the same scene before then is dropped (`openGuard` in `src/input/openGuard.ts`, which `openOverlay` and `openHomePage` go through). Every full-screen page and overlay closes from the same top-right control (`src/ui/closeControl.ts`). A card's title shares its band with that control (the detail cards, the install steps, Settings, the Logbook and the Pet shop: `addCardTitle` in `src/scenes/homePanels.ts`): it is centred between the band's left edge and the control's face, with the same clear space each side, at least 6px; a title too wide for that starts 6px in and runs to 4px short of the face (`src/ui/barTitle.ts`). A band with no close control, such as the purchase confirm's, centres its title across the band.

## Dev tools

In a browser tab, `/play/` reads a run setup from the URL; invalid values, unknown ids included, are warned about and ignored. A URL with any run parameter (all but `dev` and `route`) skips the home screen and starts the walk, its pack and gear replacing the saved loadout for that walk only. A walk started past zone 1 banks no money and sets no best walk, its zone counts leave out the zones it skipped, and a finished one counts as headed home. Reaching the home screen removes every parameter in the table but `dev` from the URL, leaving any other.

| Param    | Value                                                       | Default        |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| `dev`    | `1` shows crash details and sets the `dogWalkerGame` global | `0`            |
| `gear`   | a gear id, or `none`                                        | saved loadout  |
| `money`  | whole cents                                                 | starting purse |
| `pack`   | 1 to 3 dog ids, comma-separated                             | saved loadout  |
| `route`  | zone ids walked, from the first                             | pre-fork route |
| `seed`   | integer 0 to 4294967295                                     | random         |
| `speed`  | scroll-speed multiplier                                     | 1              |
| `walker` | a walker id                                                 | saved walker   |
| `zone`   | 1 to the walk's length                                      | 1              |

- **Build label:** the Dev bar, the crash screen's dev details and the foot of Settings show `v<version> · <commit>` (`BUILD_LABEL` in `vendor/game-kit/src/build/buildLabel.ts`), from `__APP_VERSION__` (package.json) and `__COMMIT__`, which `vite.config.ts` defines from `WORKERS_CI_COMMIT_SHA`, else `git rev-parse HEAD`, else `dev` (`vendor/game-kit/src/build/buildVersion.ts`).
- **Dev panel:** a "Dev" bar below the canvas opens the cheat sheet; the page starts `#dev-panel` hidden and mounting the panel shows it. Its top section, "Changes your walks", holds Start at and the cheats (Invincible owners, Show dev stats, Show hit boxes, Movement speed: slow, normal or fast), each applying on its own, staged until Apply; applied cheats last for the tab (`sessionStorage`) and never reach the save. Apply turns dev mode (`dev=1`) on exactly when a cheat is in use or Start at picks a walk; with every setting at its default, it loads the start screen without `dev` and the run overrides. Below a divider, the save cheats (+$500, +$2,500, Unlock all, Reset save, after a confirmation) act at once: they are queued in `sessionStorage`, written at the next boot before the save loads, and reload to the start screen. Under them, a line says whether the browser keeps the game's storage (Persistent storage, under Phone).
- **Input log:** lane moves, card choices and heading home are recorded with their step's time in a versioned log (a replay rejects a log of any other version), whose setup names the pack, gear, walker and route, so a replay does not depend on the saved loadout. RunScene writes the finished run's log to `sessionStorage` key `dog-walker-game:dev-last-log` and the next walk started plays a log found under `dog-walker-game:dev-replay`. A replay writes nothing to the save: no checkpoint and no banked run, and it ends on the home screen.
- **Overlay:** shows on a walk while the Show dev stats cheat is on: the seed, zone, distance, speed, money, hearts and FPS, and a zone clock (`src/dev/zoneClock.ts`) that splits the zone's wall-clock time into road time, held time and lag. C copies a link that reproduces the run.
- **Random streams:** one seed feeds `spawns-<zone>` (which also draws the zone's gear drops), `movers-<zone>`, `ambient`, `cards`, `distractions`, `forks` and `quips`, where `<zone>` is the position in the walk. `movers-<zone>` takes the mover draws `PlannerStreams` (`src/runner/moverPlanning.ts`) lists and a stepper's steps past its least, so they never shift `spawns-<zone>`. `cards` draws a dog's dodges (a dog with no dodge chance draws nothing) and each fork's upgrades.
