# Tech Plan

## Platform and delivery

- **Platforms:** web, phone browser first. iOS and Android later by wrapping the web build (Capacitor).
- **Engine:** Phaser 4 + TypeScript, built with Vite into static files.
- **Repo:** private GitHub repository `andornaut/dog-walker-game`.
- **Hosting:** Cloudflare Workers static assets, no Worker script.
- **Deploys:** Cloudflare Workers Builds deploys every push to `main`, running `npm run build:deploy`, which gates the deploy on the type check and the tests. A push that changes only `**/*.md`, `art/**` or `gameplay/**` does not deploy (Workers Builds watch paths).
- **Tooling:** follows `andornaut/cloudflare-starter`: Node 24, Vitest, ESLint with the shared house rules, Prettier, markdownlint, husky + lint-staged, Dependabot, and the Test and AI attributions workflows.
- **Type checking:** `strict`, `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, `noImplicitOverride`, `exactOptionalPropertyTypes` and `noFallthroughCasesInSwitch`.

## Site

`/` is a static home page, `/play/` the game, `/data/` every game data table plus simulated runs, and `/art/` the art gallery (copied from `public/art/`). The HTML pages are Vite build inputs in `pages/`, Vite's root, each at its URL's path (`pages/play/index.html` is `/play/`). They load modules as `/src/...`, which `vite.config.ts` aliases to the repository's `src/`.

## Architecture

- **Rendering:** 360×560 internal, portrait, scaled by the largest whole number of device pixels per game pixel that fits, centred and letterboxed, with `pixelArt`. Every game UI element is drawn in the canvas; HTML is only the page shell, the dev panel and the crash screen.
- **Crash screen:** `src/crash/` shows it once, on the first uncaught error or unhandled rejection, and ignores extension, cross-origin (`Script error.`) and ResizeObserver-loop errors. Phaser does not catch errors in its step, so they reach `window` and stop its loop. It pauses the game, writes nothing (the next launch banks the last zone checkpoint) and offers Reload; with `dev=1` it adds the message, stack and a Copy button.
- **Screen layout:** from the top, each across the full width: the walk bar 360×48 (cash, the neighbourhood's name and its "n of N" place in the walk, the effect wells, and Home, Help, Sound, Settings and Pause), the backdrop band 360×158 (the zone's neighbourhood and scenery gags, never part of a lane), the road, and a row of 3 dog cards 120×74 on the card band. A bar per active debuff lies over the band's top under the walk bar. The road's 5 lanes are 64px wide at the pack (x 20 to 340), with verges and sidewalks out to the screen's edges. Every rect comes from `SCREEN_CONFIG` through `screenLayout` (`src/layout/screenLayout.ts`), and the walk bar's wells and buttons, the debuff bars, a card's insides and the owner's tab from `src/hud/walkHud.ts`. A fired dog's card greys, so the cards never move.
- **Pseudo-3D road:** game logic keeps the flat road (lane x, distance ahead of the walker in road pixels, scale 1 at the pack); only drawing goes through `src/layout/projection.ts` (`ROAD_CAMERA` in `src/data/screen.ts`). The road and verge are drawn row by row into one canvas texture (`src/layout/roadRaster.ts`), with 2x and 4x shrunk strips for rows that cover more than 2 strip rows. Sprites snap to one of 5 depth steps (1, 0.875, 0.75, 0.625, 0.5) and use the manifest's `<key>@88`, `@75`, `@62` or `@50` variant when loaded. The camera is derived so the far edge sits on the band's bottom edge at scale 0.5 with `seconds.lookAheadSeconds` of road in view at the base speed, read once at load; spawns come into view there. The backdrops paint the road's approach to their cross street in the band's bottom `roadIntoBand` rows: the raster draws the moving road surface over them too, beside the backdrop's own verges.
- **Hit and draw sizes:** the pack collides as it is drawn: its parts' hit rectangles are the walker's and each dog's opaque pixels, measured on the art, 2 px (`PART_INSET`) inside them, and its outline is their bounding box with no inset, leashes included (`packParts` and `packOutline` in `src/layout/packFormation.ts`). An obstacle hits a part rectangle as the obstacle is drawn: its hit box is the bounding box of its sprites' opaque pixels at its depth, `PART_INSET` inside them on every side (`obstacleHitBox` in `src/runner/walk.ts`, from `drawnObstacleBox` in `src/runner/obstacleBox.ts` and the silhouettes in `src/layout/obstacleArt.ts`), cut to its lanes as the road is drawn on each part's row, so a pack settled in another lane is never touched. Its item height is that box's length along the road as it leaves the pack's band, the outline's rows (`packBand`; `itemHeight` in `src/runner/walk.ts`), which the bots and pulls read, and the planner gives every footprint row the longest any obstacle runs (`plannedCellInset`), widening the rows past `spawnRowSpacing` when a row's blocking length needs it; its pack-fit gap is the longest any pack's outline runs (`tallestPackLength`). Pickups and lucky finds touch the outline with the hit size, `ROAD_HIT_SIZE` (40 px; `src/assets/spriteKeys.ts`): their lane less its spare width beside it, and `itemHeights` (`src/runner/walkGeometry.ts`). Drawing uses the sprite size, `ROAD_SPRITE_SIZE` (48 px), for placeholders and fallback metrics; loaded art draws at its own frame size from the manifest.
- **Walk session:** `src/runner/session.ts` is the walk's one step order, pure and shared by `RunScene` and the simulator. A step applies its inputs (lane moves, heading home), then the pulls, the Zoomies' drift and the walk; it queues the step's cards, an owner who fired you holds the walk for `seconds.firingHoldSeconds` as a hit stop does, in a stop marked `firing` that the scene draws with the pack, the road's movers and the ambient movers standing still (the firing moment's face, plate, fired line and trot-off end with it, `firingTimeline` in `src/hud/moments.ts`, and stop whenever the walk does), then the next card opens, or the zone ends (fees, then the fork, each path carrying an upgrade), or the next zone is planned once they are chosen. Its random streams are named from the run's seed (`runStreams`; each zone's spawns from `spawns-<zone index>`), so a seed and an input log replay the same walk wherever they are played. It returns events the scene plays as sounds, bursts and labels and the simulator counts.
- **Scenes:** `RunScene` starts the walk, wires its parts and steps the walk session at a fixed 1/60 s, as many steps a frame as the wall-clock time since the last frame holds (from the frame timestamps, not Phaser's smoothed and clamped delta), up to a second of steps, so a slow device keeps to real time and a replay matches the run step for step. It steps only while `walkPaused` (`src/hud/topBar.ts`) says nothing pauses the walk: a card, the zone celebration, the pause menu and what opens from it, the Home question, a press held to explain a HUD element, a hidden tab or a window out of focus. Each overlay reports to `MenuFlow.pause()`, so a new one pauses the walk too. The parts live in `src/scenes/run/`, one concern each: the backdrop and its gags, the projected road, the items and movers on it, the roadside props, the pack and its signals, input, the walk's moments (sounds, bursts, hit moments, labels, quips, hints, badge lines), the card and pause menus, and the dev hooks (input log, replay, overlay state). `HudScene` draws the walk bar, the debuff bars and the dog cards, pops an owner's photo tab in on their dog's card (`src/scenes/ownerTabs.ts`) with their quip beside it, and stays visible during cards. Its message strip (`src/hud/messageStrip.ts`, drawn by `src/scenes/messageStripView.ts`) runs across the top of the backdrop band under the walk bar with first-time hints, badges earned on the way and the walk's events: one line at a time at 2x with its icon, wrapped onto up to two rows, on a panel as tall as its rows, fading out; under debuff bars it shows one row under the last bar. While a press holds an element up, its explanation (`src/hud/explain.ts`) takes the strip. The run scene says a line with `sayMessage(scene, icon, text)`. Under a card or the pause menu the strip draws over their dim. `CardScene` shows 2 or 3 cards and `PauseScene` the pause menu over the dimmed backdrop band and road (`walkView`, redrawn two steps down each colour's ramp), and the HUD takes no taps under them. `CardScene` reports the choice to `RunScene`. After each zone but the last, once the fee coins land, `ZoneCelebrationScene` opens over the paused run scene (`src/hud/zoneCelebration.ts` picks Zone done or New walker) and the fork card follows it. `StartScene` is the home screen; `ShopScene`, the only place to buy, opens over it below its top bar, so the bank stays in view and redraws after each purchase.
- **Text:** bitmap fonts `font-body`, `font-title`, `font-display` and `font-display-cream`, tinted to palette colours, and `font-comic` for the hit bursts' comic words, drawn untinted. Body text is drawn at 2x; text that doesn't fit is shortened, wrapped, or moved to the enlarged card.
- **Input:** keys (arrows, A/D or H/L) move one lane per press, on the key going down; a held key's auto-repeats do nothing. Touch is a relative drag that steps as the finger moves, not on release: the first lane step comes at `swipeStartDistance` of horizontal travel from where the press started and the Nth at N lane-widths, and travelling back `swipeStartDistance` from the furthest point turns the drag around and counts again from there. Each step moves the target lane by one, and the pack moves toward it lane by lane, so a step made during a lane move waits for it. Lane moves go to the next fixed step. The pack's lane slide is drawn as of the frame's time, from the current step with the queued moves applied (`leadWalk` in `src/scenes/run/interpolate.ts`), so a move shows on the next frame; the rest of the road blends between the last two steps. A tap moves one lane toward the tapped half of the road (split at its centre), the dog cards included; a tap on the walk bar or a debuff bar does nothing, and the walk bar's buttons never move the pack. Home pauses and asks whether to head home, Help and Settings open help and the settings with the walk paused under them, Pause the pause menu; the sound button and M mute or unmute. A still press on an effect icon, a debuff bar or a dog card held `seconds.explainHoldSeconds` within `swipeStartDistance` explains it in the message strip and pauses the walk until it lifts; one that moves further steers as any drag does, except on the walk bar, which never steers. A hit stop drops the drag's lane moves but not the drag. In the walk the latest finger down steers, so a thumb resting on a card never holds the steering; every other scene follows the first finger down until it lifts. A press the browser cancels picks nothing. A tap on the dimmed area outside the Settings panel closes it, and outside the pause menu resumes the walk, as the close control and Resume do, and so does a tap on the bare page beside the game (`src/input/pageTap.ts`: the page's root, body and the game's container, never the site nav or the dev panel); a press that travels past the tap distance does nothing.
- **Pulls:** `src/runner/pulls.ts`: every dog pulls toward squirrels, and only toward them. A darting squirrel is a target from its cue, in any lane; any other while it is wholly ahead of the pack and off the pack's lane. One dog strains at a time, the one whose part of the pack is nearest the squirrel across the road (the first in pack order on a tie); each squirrel draws its pulls once (`squirrelPulls` modifiers, such as Good leash, scale them). The strain runs `seconds.pullTell` with its bubble, dotted line and arrow, then the pack moves one lane toward the squirrel, with no lane lock after it. Steering away from the squirrel during the strain cancels the pull and its pulls: the pack's lane moving away, or, from an edge lane, a press toward the edge (`stepPulls` takes the step's lane presses from the session). `pullPose` gives the dog's tell while it strains and its pull lean for one lane move after. Fairness: if an obstacle, or a mover anywhere in its sweep, reaches the pack in the lane the pull would move into within the pull's lane move, `seconds.pullReactionWindow` and a lane move back, the dog strains without pulling (the blocked mark) and pulls once the lane clears; a blocked strain whose target passes ends with the pull-fail burst.
- **Movers:** an obstacle may name a `movement`: `approach` (1 or 2 steps toward the pack, each to the spot a lane aside, a row down or diagonally down nearest the pack, `seconds.moverStepSeconds` each, the second with no cue of its own), `dart` (across the road at `counts.dartLanesPerSecond`, its `tilt` drifting it down or up the road), `hop` (one jump of 1 to `most` spots one way, lanes to either side or, unless `sideways`, a row up the road or diagonally up, landing `seconds.moverHopSeconds` after it leaves, 0.3 s while the tuning has none), `inLane` (down its lane at `factor` of the road's speed), `step` (`steps` spots, each a lane or a row in any direction, `seconds.moverStepSeconds` each) or `still`. Each obstacle drawn with a movement comes as a mover at the zone's `moverShare`, and meets the pack as its kind does. `src/runner/movers.ts` plans each mover's whole path from the zone's seed, so the game and the simulator's bot see the same moves; every move shows its cue `seconds.moverWarningSeconds` ahead (the edge sign and arrow for a dart, the arrow down or up a lane for an in-lane move faster or slower than the road, the wander mark before a step or hop). A hopper's planner offers every hop it can make, and the zone's seed picks among those that keep the road passable; the pack-fit gap counts every spot a hop passes over. The spawn planner (`planSpawns` in `src/runner/spawning.ts`) places the obstacles that open a card first (`opensACard` in `src/runner/walk.ts`), movers before standing ones, then the other movers, then the other spawns, so a card is the last thing skipped. Movers keep to at most `counts.maxMovers` on screen at once (of them at most `counts.maxDartMovers` darting and `counts.maxSlowVehicles` in-lane slower than the road), each blocking every lane it can touch for as long as it can overlap the pack and keeping the pack-fit gap (`packFitsBetween`) wherever it stands. A mover with no room to move waits for a later row with room, or is skipped; one that opens a card stands still in its footprint instead. `src/scenes/run/moverView.ts` plays the first of its movement's tags (`MOVING_TAGS`) that its sprite has, else its idle tag, mirrored while it faces left (`facesLeft`: a dart running left, or a stepper or hopper from a step left until a step right), since side-view art faces right. Road art not drawn yet draws the sprite `ART_STAND_INS` names for it (`src/assets/spriteKeys.ts`) until its own key is in the manifest.
- **Ambient movers:** cars and bikes on the backdrop's cross street, people on the sidewalks beside the road, and animals darting, hopping or flying off across the cross street's sidewalks and the lawns or paving beside the road, never on it (`src/road/ambient.ts`, drawn by `src/road/phaser/ambientView.ts`). Each zone's mix is a table in `src/data/ambient.ts`: each kind's sprite key, moving tag, path, gait, speed and how often one sets out per minute. They collide with nothing and change nothing in play, and draw from the run's `ambient` stream, which the session never reads, so a seed plays the same walk with or without them. They keep the walk's time, so they hold while it is held; at most `AMBIENT_LIMIT` show at once, from a pool of sprites made once; reduced motion shows none. A mover whose art is missing plays the sprite and tag `AMBIENT_FALLBACKS` (`src/assets/spriteKeys.ts`) names for it, or is not shown.
- **Sizes:** dogs differ by size only: an owner's heart cap is `hearts.maxBySize` and every big dog slows the pack's lane changes by `multipliers.bigDogAgility` (`src/rules/dogs.ts`), and a small dog dodges a plain hit landing on it at `probability.smallDodgeChance` (`src/runner/walk.ts`).
- **Hit cues:** a plain or moving hit and a contact (a puddle) play their cues from `src/layout/hitEffects.ts`, drawn by `src/scenes/run/hitFx.ts` at `DEPTH.fx`: the bump (`fx-bump`, a mover's `fx-bump-big`) centred over the struck dog's top row with the obstacle's debris (`OBSTACLE_HITS`) just over it, the struck dog's `react` and white fill flash, the obstacle's motion (a cone tips over, a bin rocks, a wide obstacle shakes sideways, a mover jolts back up the road), and a mover's 2 px shake of the run scene's camera, the HUD's excepted. A contact's splash sits on the touching dog's feet (`ContactHit.dog`), with its edge flash, the walker's `stumble` and every dog's `react` and `shake`. Reduced motion holds each bump's or splash's peak frame for the stop, blinks the flash once and skips the rest. A hit obstacle leaves play at once, but `ItemViews` keeps its sprites as scenery (`src/road/struckScenery.ts`): it scrolls with the road, a mover stopped where it was hit, under every live road sprite and the pack (`DEPTH.struck`), until it passes the road's bottom or the zone changes. Scenery collides with nothing and the walk never sees it, so a seed plays the same walk.
- **Accessibility:** reduced motion (a setting, defaulting to `prefers-reduced-motion`) drops decorative motion but keeps every highlight and state change, the road scroll and the lane slide. No state relies on colour alone. Tap targets are at least 44 game pixels (see Phone). Text meets 4.5:1 contrast and essential icons 3:1.
- **Interaction states:** every tile, tab, card option and button shows one state from `src/ui/interaction.ts` (`tileState`, `buttonState`), drawn as `art/style-guide.md` sets out: disabled (locked, an unpicked dog when the pack is full, or otherwise unavailable; flat grey, no lift, its pictures from `disabledTextureKey`), normal (cream), selected (gold, a 2px lift and `ui-check`) or primary (the screen's one call to action, the green go button). A walker, dog or Pocket gear tile takes its state from `itemTile`: available, picked, pack full (owned and unpicked while a pack of 2 or more slots is full; its art grey), locked (not owned; a flat navy silhouette from `silhouetteTextureKey`, `icon-lock` and a price plate, green when the bank covers it, dark red when not) or locked by a goal (a walker not yet unlocked: a plate with `homeText.lockedName`, and a tap says its goal). `ui-afford` outlines only the Pet shop building and the shop's rows. Walker, dog and gear tiles are `ui-tile`; tabs draw their face in code inside `ui-select`; a Settings switch row is normal and shows its setting in its switch. Each piece has a drawn fallback while its art is missing.
- **Sound:** `src/audio/` maps cues to manifest keys and mixes music and effects volumes from settings, ducking music to `PAUSED_MUSIC_DUCK` and muting effects while the player has paused, and muting both while hidden. `src/scenes/soundboard.ts` is the Phaser wrapper. Each sound ships as Ogg plus AAC.
- **Source layout:** each context keeps its pure code at its root and the code that imports Phaser in a `phaser/` subfolder. A moved module leaves a one-line re-export at its old path until its importers move to the new one.
  - `src/lib/`: helpers with no game knowledge (arrays, `assertNever`, seeded random streams, rects, text lines)
  - `src/data/`: content and tuning
  - `src/rules/`, `src/state/`: run state and pure rules
  - `src/runner/`: the walk session, lane motion, zones, spawn planning, collisions, the hit stop, pulls
  - `src/input/`: keys and the touch gesture state machine
  - `src/home/`, `src/hud/`, `src/help/`: view models for the home, settings, stats and end screens, the walk bar and pause menu, and the help screen
  - `src/ui/`: the pixel-art UI kit: the pixel font, text fitting, grids, card rows, close controls, badges, pixel shapes, and tile and button states; `phaser/` draws pixel and display text, panels, the palette and the dimmed ramps
  - `src/layout/`: screen geometry
  - `src/pack/`: how every screen draws the pack: each leash from the walker's hand to the dog's collar, and the draw order (the dogs by their feet rows, then the leashes, then the walker); `phaser/` stands the walker, the dogs, their leashes and shadows for the walk, the start screen's gate and the end screen's walk home, so the three draw it alike
  - `src/road/`: the pseudo-3D road: clipping sprites at the juncture and what lies beside the road; `phaser/` draws the backdrop band and its gags
  - `src/assets/`: manifest and Aseprite parsing, art keys and depth steps; `atlas/` packs the atlases at build time, and `phaser/` loads the manifest's art and makes its grey, dark and silhouette textures
  - `src/save/`: persistence
  - `src/sim/`: the run simulator
  - `src/pwa/`, `src/dev/`: phone app and dev tools
  - `src/dataPage/`: the `/data/` page
  - `src/headers/`: the build-time security headers
  - `src/site/`: tests of the site nav's stylesheet, `public/nav.css` (the current page's frame holds still)
  - `src/tools/`: the art manifest, the data export for art's tools and the depth preview
  - `src/crash/`: the crash screen
  - `src/scenes/`, `src/main.ts`: everything that touches Phaser; `src/scenes/run/` holds the walk's views, input, moments, menus and dev hooks
- **Boundary:** only `src/main.ts`, `src/scenes/` and the contexts' `phaser/` folders may import Phaser or code that does, and only tests may import `src/testing/`. Each context imports only the folders its row in `CONTEXTS` (`eslint.config.mjs`) lists: `src/lib/` imports nothing from `src/`. ESLint enforces all three, so all game logic runs in unit tests without a renderer.
- **Assets:** the art agent exports into `public/art/`. `public/art/manifest.json` lists `sprites` (PNG plus Aseprite hash JSON), `fonts` (XML BMFont), `images` and `sounds`, with paths relative to `public/art/`; Valibot validates it and the sprite JSON at boot. Keys are kebab-case and unique. Dog-keyed art (`dog-<art>` and its depth variants, `face-dog-<art>`, `portrait-dog-<art>`, `face-owner-<art>`, `owner-<art>` and the `ui-dog-card` tag) names the dog by the `art` id in its data, so a renamed dog keeps its art until the art is renamed. Zone-keyed art (`backdrop-<art>`, `road-<art>`, `road-<art>-start`, `icon-zone-<art>`, `card-zone-<art>` and the `ui-zone-pips` tags `<art>` and `<art>-done`) names the zone by its `art` id the same way. Animation keys are `<texture>:<tag>`. Sprites anchor on the feet, and a missing key draws a placeholder box. Art rules live in `art/style-guide.md`.
  - **Depth steps:** `shared/depth-steps.json` is the one table of a road sprite's depth steps, read by the game and by art's tools: each step's key suffix and exact scale (1 with no suffix, `@88` 0.875, `@75` 0.75, `@62` 0.625, `@50` 0.5), and the size rule for a variant's frame, `floor(full * scale + 0.5)`. The manifest accepts exactly these suffixes, and a road sprite draws from the smallest step it has at least its projected size.
  - **Palette:** `shared/palette.json` is generated by `python3 -I art/tools/pixel.py palette`: every house palette colour by name, its hex and the name of the colour one step down its ramp. `src/assets/housePalette.ts` reads it, and `src/assets/darkRamp.ts` darkens through it.
  - **Verges:** `shared/verges.json` is generated by `python3 -I art/tools/backdrops.py verges --write`: what lies beside each zone's road, keyed by the zone's `art` id, with colours by palette name (the curb, strips, sidewalk or paving with its seams and dots, and the lawn with its base, mown stripe or rough patch and tuft colours). `src/road/roadside.ts` reads it into each zone's `Verge`; a null `walkTo` paves out to the screen's edges, and `lawnColors` is null exactly when there is no lawn.
  - **Roadside table:** `shared/roadside.json` is generated: it is what `python3 -I art/tools/roadside.py table` prints, never edited by hand, and a test fails when the two differ. It places each zone's roadside props (keyed by the zone's `art` id) along both verges: per side, a slot every `slot` road px alternating a lot and a gap from the table's `lots` and `gaps` by each side's start and stride, each prop at `across` from the centre line (the left side mirrored to negative across, the art itself never flipped) and `dz` from its slot. `src/road/roadsideProps.ts` places them through the projection from the feet to the far edge, none on a zone's start strip, under every road sprite and ambient mover (`DEPTH.roadside`), with each zone's ground shadow (its `shadowColour`) under the keys in `shadowed`. They are cosmetic: no collisions, no random numbers, not in the simulator.
  - **Generated manifest:** `public/art/manifest.json` is derived from the files and committed (`src/tools/artManifest.ts`). A key is its file's name less the extension, and its kind is its folder: `sprites/<key>.png` plus `.json`, `images/<key>.png`, `fonts/<key>.fnt` with the PNG its one page names, `sounds/<key>.ogg` plus `.m4a`. Each sprite `standsOnGround` picks gets an anchor from frame 0 by `spriteAnchor` (its last opaque row and opaque width), the rule boot uses for a sprite without one. `public/art/manifest.extra.json` holds what a file cannot say: `{"sounds": {"<key>": {"loop": true}}}`. A build writes the manifest before packing the atlases, the dev server rewrites it as the art changes, and `npm run art:manifest` writes it.
  - **Data export:** `npm run export:data` writes `shared/export/game-data.json` (the world and its zones, dogs, walkers, encounters, minor obstacles, upgrades, gear, debuffs, every card face with its effect text, the tuning and the help pages, each with its art keys) and `shared/export/layout.json` (the screen regions, lanes, walk bar, HUD lanes, message strip, dog cards, camera, the road on the band's approach rows, depth steps, road sprite sizes, pack formations, scenery gags, comic words and each scene's panels) from the game's modules, for art's tools. `npm run build` runs it first and the dev server reruns it as `src/` changes.
  - **Depth preview:** `npm run art:depth-preview -- <key> [zone]` draws a road sprite through the walk's projection and `depthDraw` at a sweep of distances on each zone's road, writing PNGs and a contact sheet of the step and scale picked at each distance to `out/depth-preview/<key>/`.
  - **Art checks:** `npm run check:art` runs only the art tests (`src/assets/`, `src/scenes/artCoverage.test.ts`, `src/tools/artManifest.test.ts`), for checking an export locally; they fail when the committed manifest differs from what the files give, as does `npm run art:manifest -- --check`. A test fails when `shared/export/` is stale. `npm run art:unused` lists the manifest keys that no source file or game data names, for information only. A build prints each atlas page's size.
- **Atlases:** a build packs every image and sprite frame into a few atlas pages (`src/assets/atlas/atlasPlugin.ts`): at most 2048 px a side, grouped `boot` (the loading screen), `ui`, `home` (the road art the home screen draws too: each dog, owner and walker at full size, the coin and a few effects), `run` and `cards` (the choice cards), each packed rect's edge pixels extruded 1 px, written to `/art/atlas/<group>-<n>.png` with `/art/atlas-manifest.json` mapping each key and frame to its page and rect. A `home` or `run` sprite's frames are trimmed to their opaque pixels, each recording its offset in the whole frame (Phaser's `setTrim`), so origins, flips, crops and anchors measure the whole frame; UI art, cards and images stay whole, since tile sprites, nine-slices and the road's backdrop reads treat a packed rect as the whole frame. A sprite's frames with the same packed pixels share one rect. BootScene loads the `boot` page first, then the `ui` and `home` pages, the fonts and the sounds the home screen and its menus play (`HOME_CUES`), shows the home screen, and loads the `run` and `cards` pages and the other sounds behind it; Start walk and help wait for those (Start walk shows held down, dimmed and labelled `homeText.startLoading` meanwhile), and a dev link that names a run loads everything first. `src/scenes/homeArt.test.ts` reads the home screen's and its menus' source and fails for an art key or sound they name that boot leaves until later. BootScene gives each key its own texture whose frames are rects of the shared page, so texture keys, frame names, `<texture>:<tag>` animations and anchors work unchanged. The loading bar weighs each file by its size, a page by its `bytes` in the atlas manifest, so a few large pages move it steadily. A key the atlas leaves out, and every key on the dev server, loads from its own files. The art files under `public/art/` are copied as the art agent exports them.
- **Tests:** Vitest over all pure logic; scenes are checked by playing and by headless screenshots.
- **Simulator:** `src/sim/` plays whole runs on the real rules over a matrix of card policies (hearts unless an owner would drop to 1, always hearts, always pay, or mixed: pay about half the time from its own seeded stream, and whenever an owner would drop to 1), route policies (the path taken at each fork: straight, left to the easiest, right to the hardest, or random; straight by default), players, gear (none by default), packs and walkers (the default walker by default). A player is a steering bot of a skill preset (novice, typical, expert in `src/sim/skills.ts`: reaction delay, look-ahead, error rate, lane-change cooldown), which plays the pure walk loop (`stepWalk` with pulls and movers, no renderer) and plans lane changes over what it has seen; its hit rates are measured, and the Forced column is the share of hits that came within a pull's fairness horizon from outside the lane it pulled the pack out of. The bot plays the game's own walk session at the game's 1/60 s step: its lane moves are the steps' inputs and the policies choose each card as it opens, so a run's seed and its input log (`RunResult.inputs`) replay it in the game. `npm run simulate -- [--skill novice,typical,expert] [--policy ...] [--route ...] [--gear none,fannyPack,...] [--walker casey,ben,ada,trista] [--runs 1000] [--seed 1] [--out rows.json]`; `--help` lists the columns. Results are deterministic per seed: each run's seed names the game's streams as the game names them, and the bot's errors and the policies' choices draw from streams of their own (`bot`, `policy`). The Badges column counts the achievements a run earns on its own facts. Balance changes are compared before and after at the same seed.

## Game data

- **Card text:** generated from the effects and tuning, covered by a snapshot test.
- **Choices:** `cardFor` builds a card (text, affected dogs, options disabled when unaffordable); `chooseOption` applies a choice and returns the new run state plus the outcome events the scenes animate.
- **Effects:** card options are lists of effects from a closed set of kinds, naming money amounts and modifiers by their `tuning.ts` key. Hearts deltas are written inline. A new kind is code in `src/rules/` plus a test.
- **Format:** typed TypeScript modules in `src/data/`. Content is `as const satisfies Type`. Tuning uses plain `satisfies` so its values stay `number` for live tuning.
- **Integrity:** `src/data/data.test.ts` checks the rulebook's invariants, and type-level tests show invalid data fails to compile.
- **Live tuning:** editing a value file under the dev server updates the running game through Vite HMR. Scenes and rules read tuning at the time of use.
- **Ownership:** gameplay edits values and content in the value files. Tech owns `types.ts`, `index.ts`, `screen.ts`, `src/rules/` and the tests.
- **Units:** money (integer cents), hearts, seconds, probability, pixels and multipliers are branded types in the rules; value files hold plain numbers. Hearts deltas are -3 to 3.
- **Zones and forks:** `world.ts` lists the zones from easiest to hardest, each with its difficulty (1 to 5 paws and a name), fee, speed and density multipliers, mover share and weights for its obstacles, minor obstacles and lucky finds (0 keeps a kind out). A walk is `walkLength` zones from `firstZone`, ending on `lastZone`. The run's `route` lists the zones walked by id, and `zoneIndex` is a position in it. After each zone but the last comes a fork card (`offerFork`, a card source of its own): up to three unwalked zones drawn at random, kept in world order so the easiest is on the left, each carrying one upgrade. The fork before the last zone leads only to `lastZone`, and no earlier fork offers it. Its choice is an ordinary card choice that takes the path's upgrade and appends the zone to the route.

## Save data

- **Scope:** one profile per browser, local only, per origin.
- **Contents:** bank (cents), owned dogs, gear, slots and walkers (a walker is owned once any walk has finished its zone: banking a run adds those its best zones finished unlock, and the free ones stay owned on every load), chosen walker (one of those owned), last loadout, stats and logbook (`gameplay/design.md`, Home screen and progression), shown hints, settings, earned achievements with the time each was earned, and the zone checkpoint (with the run's achievement counters and route), all referenced by data ids. A checkpoint or last walk saved before forks has no route and loads with the pre-fork route (Elm Avenue, Fairview Park, Main Street, The Long Way Home).
- **Format:** one JSON document under `dog-walker-game:save` with a `version` field; every write replaces it.
- **Validation:** a Valibot schema parses every load. Unknown ids are dropped. A save that fails validation is kept under a backup key and the game starts fresh.
- **Migrations:** ordered pure functions between save versions, each tested against a fixture. Renaming a content id needs one.
- **Storage:** an async interface. The web adapter wraps `localStorage`; a Capacitor `Preferences` adapter will replace it in the app, where iOS can evict web storage. When storage fails, the game stays playable and says progress won't be saved.
- **Writes:** on a purchase, a loadout, walker, settings or hint change, after each zone (the checkpoint) and at run end. An unfinished run is banked on the next launch as a run that headed home.
- **Achievements:** `src/rules/achievements.ts` judges each badge's conditions (a closed set of kinds with thresholds in the data) when a run is banked, on a purchase and on launch. Badges earned at run end celebrate on the end screen; those earned on launch or by a purchase celebrate over the home screen.
- **Scenes:** `src/scenes/profileStore.ts` holds the loaded save for every scene and is the only way scenes read or change it.

## Phone

- Audio resumes from iOS's `interrupted` or `suspended` on each return to the foreground and each gesture (`touchend` on iOS), unless the settings mute it.
- Tap targets are at least 44×44 render px, larger than their art where needed. Every full-screen page and overlay closes from the same control in its top-right corner (`src/ui/closeControl.ts`).
- Installable web app: `/manifest.webmanifest` and a service worker, `/sw.js`, built by `src/pwa/precachePlugin.ts`, precache the game, the atlas pages and atlas manifest, and every other file in the art manifest (fonts, and sounds in one format only). Boot makes at least 35 requests: the page, its scripts, the two manifests, six atlas pages, ten font files (a `.fnt` and a `.png` for each of 5 fonts) and a file per sound (15). HTML, the art manifest and the site nav's `nav.js` and `nav.css` are network-first; hashed assets, precached files and sound files are cache-first. A fresh load holds the boot for up to 4 seconds while it checks for a new version (not when offline); until the player presses a key, touches the game or starts a walk, a waiting version takes over at once (the page posts `skip-waiting` and reloads on `controllerchange`), so one that installs within the hold reloads before the game draws. After the player has interacted the page never reloads on its own: each arrival at the home screen checks for a new version, the "Update ready" toast shows there while one waits, and its Reload does the same. `updateAction` in `src/pwa/updateReady.ts` makes the decision.
- Installed app: on `/play/` in `fullscreen` or `standalone` display mode, or with iOS's `navigator.standalone`, `public/nav.js` leaves the nav bar out (and follows a display-mode change), so no link leaves the app and the game takes the full height under the top safe-area inset.
- Phone events (`PHONE_EVENTS` in `src/pwa/`): `back-pressed` pauses, `app-hidden` pauses and checkpoints, `turned-sideways` pauses, and the window losing focus pauses; each opens the pause menu, which stays when the page comes back. The run holds a screen wake lock, and the first gesture unlocks audio.
- Portrait only: the manifest locks `portrait`, and a phone on its side (coarse pointer, landscape, viewport at most 500 CSS px tall) gets a "Turn your phone upright" notice over the page.
- Security headers: `src/headers/securityHeadersPlugin.ts` writes `_headers` at build time: nosniff, referrer and permissions policies, and a CSP per path (`/`, `/play/*`, `/data/*`, `/art/*`) allowing `'self'` plus hashes of each page's inline blocks. No script injects a `<style>`, so no hash depends on a script's text: the site nav's CSS is `public/nav.css`, which each page links beside `public/nav.js`. Google Fonts is allowed only on `/` and `/art/*`. Content-hashed build output under `/assets/*` is `public, max-age=31536000, immutable`, and the build fails for an unhashed name there; every other file, `/sw.js` and the HTML included, keeps the platform's `max-age=0, must-revalidate`. Font descriptors (`/art/fonts/*.fnt`) are served as `text/xml`, which Cloudflare compresses; it serves an unknown extension untyped and uncompressed.
- Sound format: each sound ships as Ogg and AAC (`.m4a`). The page picks the first the browser plays, Ogg before AAC (`src/audio/soundFormat.ts`, asking `canPlayType` the same types Phaser checks). BootScene loads only that file, and the page registers `/sw.js?sound=<format>` so the worker precaches only that format; a worker has no `canPlayType` of its own. A sound the install did not store is cached on its first fetch.
- The canvas takes no browser gestures (`touch-action: none`).

## Dev tools

`/play/` reads a run setup from the URL. Invalid values are warned about and ignored. A renamed dog id is warned about and replaced by its new one, in a URL and in a replayed log's setup. A URL with any run parameter (all but `dev` and `route`) skips the home screen and starts the walk; the setup's pack and gear replace the saved loadout for that walk without changing it, and the result is banked as usual. The overrides (every parameter but `dev`) apply only to the walk they start: reaching the home screen removes them from the URL (`history.replaceState`, keeping the path and other parameters) and from the page's setup, so the start screen's picks decide every later walk.

| Param    | Value                           | Default         |
| -------- | ------------------------------- | --------------- |
| `seed`   | integer 0 to 4294967295         | random          |
| `pack`   | 1 to 3 dog ids, comma-separated | saved loadout   |
| `gear`   | a gear id, or `none`            | saved loadout   |
| `walker` | a walker id                     | saved walker    |
| `zone`   | 1 to the walk's length          | 1               |
| `route`  | zone ids walked, from the first | pre-fork route  |
| `money`  | whole cents                     | gear's starting |
| `speed`  | scroll-speed multiplier         | 1               |
| `dev`    | `1` shows the overlay           | `0`             |

- `route` lists the zones from the first up to at least `zone`; a later `zone` without it starts on the pre-fork route.
- One seed feeds independent random streams: `spawns-<zone>`, `ambient`, `cards`, `forks`, `quips` and `scenery`, where `<zone>` is the position in the walk. `cards` also draws a lucky find's effects, a small dog's dodges and each fork's upgrades. Pulls draw nothing: each follows from the road, so the input log needs nothing for them.
- Lane moves, card choices (a fork's path among them) and heading home are recorded with their simulation step's time in a versioned input log that replays the run exactly. The log's setup names the pack, gear and walker walked, and a later start's route, so a replay does not depend on the saved loadout. RunScene writes the finished run's log to `sessionStorage` key `dog-walker-game:dev-last-log` and plays a log found under `dog-walker-game:dev-replay`.
- The overlay (backquote) shows the seed, zone, distance, speed, money, hearts and FPS; C copies a link that reproduces the run. Its zone clock (`src/dev/zoneClock.ts`) splits the current zone's wall-clock time into road time (simulated, the road moving), held time (hit stops and firings in simulated time, cards and the pause menu in wall-clock time) and lag (the rest: the loop behind real time), beside the road time the whole zone takes, and keeps the last zone's totals.
- The far-road fade dithers the road's farthest rows into the band. It is off unless the game registry's `dev-road-fade` is set, as through the console handle `dogWalkerGame`.
- The dev panel is the cheat sheet for a person testing, under a "Dev" bar below the canvas that stays visible. Opened, the sheet sits below the game and the page scrolls to it. Every cheat is off until Cheats on. The Cheats on/off switch, Start at and the toggles are staged and change nothing until Apply, which loads the chosen start with the staged cheats; Cancel, Escape or the bar close the sheet and discard them. With Cheats off applied, every cheat is off and Apply loads the start screen without `dev` or the run parameters. The staged settings:
  - Start at: the start screen (the default), or a neighborhood walked with the saved loadout. The first zone starts the walk; any other is walked second, after the first.
  - Invincible owners: no owner loses hearts (`RunState.invincible`, which `changeHearts` reads).
  - Hit boxes: draws the pack's outline (green), its part rectangles (yellow) and each road item's hit box (red): an obstacle's (`obstacleHitBox`) as the screen rectangle it hits over, anything else's laid on the road.
  - Slow motion and Fast forward: the walk at x0.5 or x3; turning one on turns the other off.
- The save cheats act at once: +$100, +$1,000 and Unlock all (every dog, gear item, walker and slot) change the save, and Reset save writes a new player's save after a confirm. Each is queued in `sessionStorage` and written at the next boot, before the game loads the save, and the page reloads to the start screen.
- Applied cheats last for the tab (`sessionStorage`) and never reach the save.
