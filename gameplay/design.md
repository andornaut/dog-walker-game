# Game Design

The rulebook for the first complete game, set in one world, Toronto. What's left to build and tune is in [`README.md`](README.md), what comes later is in [`next.md`](next.md), and undecided ideas are in [`ideas.md`](ideas.md).

[`src/data/`](../src/data/) holds every number and all content (names, text, prices, shapes, weights), and the [data page](https://dogwalker.tristamittermayer.com/data/) shows them with the generated card text. This doc states the rules, with numbers where they define one. `$` and `♥` give the direction and rough size of a change (`$$$` and `♥♥` are bigger).

## Pitch

A cozy pixel-art lane runner: you are a dog walker walking a pack of dogs through the neighbourhood to make money.

## Pillars

1. **Cozy, not frantic:** calm speed, forgiving failure, "one more walk" appeal; playable for long stretches without stress
2. **Bumps add up:** a bump costs a heart and a card obstacle brings a choice; a walk ends when you finish, head home, or the last owner fires you
3. **Charming chaos:** cute dogs, dramatic owners, silly neighbourhood

## Core loop

- **Before a walk:** pick a walker, your dogs (one per owned dog slot) and up to one owned gear item for your Pocket.
- **During a walk:** walk 4 of the 6 neighbourhoods, starting in High Park and ending on Yonge Street. The street scrolls toward you; switch lanes to dodge obstacles and grab pickups. A plain obstacle costs a heart; a card obstacle opens a choice card.
- **Between neighbourhoods:** a neighbourhood celebration that banks the pay, then a fork: each path leads to a neighbourhood and most carry an upgrade card, so one pick sets both.
- **After a walk:** bank your cash and spend your earnings at the Pet shop: dogs, dog slots and gear.

Reference points: Bowling Ballers (lane runner, but calmer), Hades (areas, run structure, loadout, meta progression), Vampire Survivors (choices along the way), Slay the Spire (choices as cards).

## Voice

- **Tone:** cozy, gentle and kid-friendly; the chaos is charming, the owners are dramatic, and no line mocks the player. Canadian spelling (colour, favourite, grey, neighbour), one name per thing (the walk's money is "cash"), spelled one way everywhere, so a name too long for its space is renamed everywhere rather than shortened in one place, ♥ right after its number ("+1♥", "{hearts}♥"), and no em dashes. Plain ASCII only, apart from ♥, · and the characters `src/ui/pixelFont.ts` maps (−, × and curly quotes): the pixel font has no other glyphs
- **Fit:** text never shrinks to fit. Every string is written to fit its box at that box's one text size; where a string can't be cut to fit, its box grows. Nothing steps a font down for a long line
- **Lengths:** message strip lines and first-time hints fit the strip's 2 rows at full size, with or without a debuff bar (312 px a row at 2×, about 36 characters); the walker's bubble about 30 characters a line, growing to fit its lines, with walker's lines and owner parting lines at 2; card option names 2 or 3 words; hit labels one or two uppercase words with "!"
- **Walkers:** Ada calm and precise, Casey warm and encouraging, Mr. B easygoing, Trista sporty and quick
- **Owners** (each with 2 lines for each of happier, upset, fired and parting):
  - **Clare** (Lady): psychologist, quiet and friendly, gentle observations, a little shy
  - **Eve** (Ginger): YouTuber, everything is a video (vlog, views, subscribers)
  - **Gadget** (Penny): nervous but kind, double-checks everything, apologizes even when firing you
  - **Jason** (Buddy): lawn care pro, laid-back, yard talk (mowing, edging, weeds)
  - **Aunt Judy** (Sequoia): tennis star, older and athletic, tennis talk (aces, faults, match point)
  - **Kiwi** (Archie): unflappable grandmother, "dear" and "pet", offers cookies and tea
  - **Ralph** (Kilo): retired pilot, big and gruff, few words, flying talk (turbulence, landings, mayday)
  - **Ryan** (Reggie): engineer, practical, specs, structures and drawing boards
  - **Ms. Tara** (Pepe): dog-show judge, curt and formal, scores and deductions, a little French

## Rules

### Screen and controls

- Web, phone browser first. Touch and keyboard; mouse is not planned
- Portrait 360×560, ¾ top-down view, 5 lanes of 64px (the road spans x 20 to 340 at the pack). The road is drawn in pseudo-3D: lanes converge toward the backdrop band and things shrink with distance, with about 3.6 seconds of road visible ahead. The road keeps a hard edge where it meets the backdrop band. Lanes, distances and collisions are unchanged by it. The game fills the screen's width or height on every device, phones and tablets alike, at any scale, and sits at the top under the status bar and any nav bar in every orientation, with all the spare height below the dog cards. On a phone or tablet held sideways the game turns with the device and stays upright for it, rather than asking the player to turn the device back; a computer with a mouse never turns it
- **Layout,** from the top: the top bar, 40 rows tall like every screen's (big cash; the effect icons in six wells: the walker's perk, the Pocket item, found gear and up to 3 upgrades, filling left to right; then Help, Sound, Settings and Pause, left to right; mute is also in Settings and on the M key); the debuff bars, when any is active; a pill naming the neighbourhood with its place in the walk ("2 of 4"), 4 rows under the last debuff bar, or under the top bar when none is active, moving as debuffs start and end; a backdrop band showing the current neighbourhood, with the message strip over its clouds, drawn over the debuff bars and the pill; the road across the full width, scrolling all the way to the top of its area; and a row of doghouse cards along the bottom, one per dog slot, with the neighbourhood progress line under them along the screen's bottom edge: a 2 px bar, bordered like the debuff bars, that fills left to right with the share of the current neighbourhood walked and starts empty again in the next. Cards, the pause menu and Settings dim the walk view (the backdrop band and the road); the top bar and doghouse cards stay lit
- **Doghouse cards:** slim cards (120×70) in pack order (the front dog, then the left and right dogs): a name header, the dog's face, the owner's face (see Owner faces) and name with their hearts, and a LEAD tag on the front dog. A fired dog's card keeps the owner's face, looking fired. The owner pops in as a photo tab when something happens to them (a heart change, the firing moment); the firing moment carries its own quip, and every other quip shows in the message strip. A still hold on a card shows its dog's size, hearts and owner
- **Pausing:** the pause menu offers Resume, Help, Settings and End walk. The walk pauses whenever something takes the player's attention: the pause menu, Settings and Help, the Home question, a card, the neighbourhood celebration, anything held for its explanation, and the tab being hidden or the window losing focus. Turning the device during a walk pauses the walk, as switching away does. Coming back to a hidden tab shows the pause menu, so the walk resumes only on Resume. While the walk is paused, everything in it holds: the road, the pack, the heart pops and other change labels, and the message strip's lines, which age only while walking. Hints and achievement toasts don't pause: they show in the message strip. While any card or overlay is open, only the walk bar's buttons respond, as they do with nothing open: the effect wells, the debuff bars and the dog cards explain themselves only with nothing open. Under an overlay that closes, a tap on them closes it as any tap outside its panel does; under a choice card or the New walker celebration, it does nothing (see Panels)
- **Hold to explain:** during a walk, holding anything that explains itself (an effect icon, a debuff bar, a dog card), or tapping an effect icon, opens its explanation in a detail card, the same one the home screen's Details show, never in the message strip, and pauses the walk. The card stays up after the finger lifts; a tap anywhere closes it and resumes the walk. A tap on a walk bar button also presses that button; any other tap does nothing else
- **End walk:** the pause menu's End walk and the fork card's End walk ask "End the walk?", showing only what ending the walk gives up, in red: what the current neighbourhood has earned, its tips, finds and pay together (none at a fork), plus the number of neighbourhoods left after this one when any are. Keep walking is the default (key 1); End walk is key 2. End walk ends the walk (see A walk) and keeps what the finished neighbourhoods earned. The pause menu's note under End walk says how much of the cash ending the walk keeps ("Ending now keeps $45 of $70", or "Ending now keeps all $70" when it keeps it all)
- **Drag** (the main touch control): put a thumb down anywhere and drag. The first lane step comes after a short drag (a quarter of a lane-width), and each further lane-width of travel adds one more, all relative to where the drag started, so the thumb never covers the walker. Dragging back turns the drag around. The pack moves lane by lane toward the target at the walker's lane-change speed
- **Tap:** moves one lane toward the tapped half of the road, as soon as the finger lands; on the doghouse row, when it lifts, so holding a card only explains it. A drag shorter than the first lane step counts as a tap, and a drag whose first step goes against the tap takes the tap back, so the pack ends where the drag alone puts it
- **Keyboard:** ←/→, A/D or H/L; one lane per press; holding a key doesn't repeat
- While moving between lanes, the pack can hit things in either lane. A reverse press turns the pack around at once; each other press queues one more lane
- Taps on the top bar never move the pack; taps and drags on the doghouse row steer like anywhere on the road. During a walk, every part of the screen below the top bar steers, the margins around the game included: a drag or tap there acts as one at the nearest edge of the game area, and the top bar and the margin beside or above it keep their own input

### The pack and hits

- **Formation:** the walker is at the bottom, the dogs close ahead in the same lane on visible leashes, every dog readable. The game sets the formation by size, never the player: the biggest dog leads in front (drawn behind the side dogs) and is the front dog, the next biggest walks on the left and the smallest on the right; ties go by roster order (alphabetical by name). Two dogs walk side by side out front, a little less far forward than a 3-dog lead, the bigger on the left as the front dog. One dog walks centred in the lead spot. No dog's hit box reaches into the next lane, so a pack fully in a lane never touches an obstacle in the lane beside it
- **Hit boxes:** one rectangle per dog and one for the walker, each a few pixels inside its sprite; leashes can't be hit. Road sprites are drawn at 48px, and each obstacle hits as drawn: 2 px inside its opaque outline, at its depth, within its own lanes. Touching any part of an obstacle counts as one hit. Once an obstacle is below the walker's feet it can no longer hit, however many rows it spans
- **Who a hit lands on:** a bump lands on the dog whose rectangle touched: if only the walker's did, on the front dog; if several dogs' did, on the one nearest the contact. Card obstacles hit the whole pack. Pickups (tips, treats and lucky finds) collect anywhere in the pack's whole outline, leashes included. Lane changes sweep the pack, so a fast move across several lanes can't skip a hazard or a pickup
- **Bumps:** running into an obstacle that opens no card is a bump. A bump costs 1♥ from the owner of the dog it lands on. A collision costs hearts, makes the pack Muddy (a puddle) or opens a card. While Spotlight lasts, a bump also costs money (see Encounters and debuffs); otherwise money is lost only through a card option you pick
- **After a hit:** the obstacle is out of play at once but stays on the road as scenery (a cone tipped over, a trash can rocking, a mover stopped) and scrolls off with it, touching nothing. Obstacles are ignored for a short grace window (about 0.7 seconds), during which the pack blinks; pickups still count
- **Hit stop:** a hit freezes the road and the pack briefly while it plays (about 1 second for a card obstacle, before its card; about 0.3 seconds for a plain obstacle or a puddle). Input is ignored during the stop, and it doesn't count as walk time. Lucky finds don't stop the walk

### Spawning

- Spawning always leaves a reachable path, accounting for every mover's moves, so every hit is avoidable. (see Spawning details)
- Scroll speed and obstacle density rise gently each neighbourhood
- Obstacles span lanes and rows (shapes in the data). None is wider than 3 lanes, every row leaves at least 2 lanes open, and obstacle rows leave room for the pack's full length.
- Every neighbourhood has fixed obstacles of all three widths: at least one 1 lane wide, one 2 lanes wide and one 3 lanes wide
- Obstacles, fixed and moving, go toward the lanes left clear longest, so no lane, the sides included, stays clear for long
- The first neighbourhood's first obstacle reaches the pack about two seconds in at the soonest, so a walk never opens with a hit

### Pulls

Every dog pulls toward squirrels. A squirrel is no hit and opens no card: touching one does nothing, it darts across as an animal mover, and the pull is its only cost. Since nothing has to steer around it, a squirrel blocks no lane: the road keeps no room clear for it, it may dart across where obstacles stand, and no tip, treat or lucky find lies on its path. A dog pulls the pack one lane only toward a squirrel whose cue has started (one waiting at the road's edge draws no pull yet) and that is still ahead of the pack, and one dog strains at a time, the one nearest the squirrel side to side. If the straining dog's owner fires you, the strain ends and that squirrel draws no more. The dog strains for about 1 second, with a bubble over it, an arrow and a line to the squirrel, and a taut leash; then the pack lurches one lane toward the squirrel, unless you steer away during the strain, which cancels the pull; in an edge lane, pressing toward the edge counts as steering away. The message strip announces the strain as its cue; a cancel is quiet. The pull goes toward the squirrel's side as it is when the pack lurches, so a squirrel that crosses changes it, and a squirrel in the pack's own lane keeps the dog straining with no pull until it moves out of that lane. A pull never moves the pack into an obstacle it can't react to: if a lane-blocking obstacle or mover in the target lane would reach the pack within about two lane moves and a moment to react, the dog keeps straining and pulls once the lane clears. The strain ends with no pull if the squirrel goes first: off the road past the edge beside the pack, into the pack's rows, or away with the straining dog when its owner fires you.

### Resources

- **Money ($):** the only currency, in whole dollars. A multiplied amount rounds to the nearest dollar
  - **Cash:** what you carry during the walk. It starts at $0 and grows with tips and lucky finds; card options are paid from it. It never goes below $0. What the walk banks depends on how it ends: a finished walk banks all its cash; heading home, or every owner firing you, banks the cash held at the end of the last finished neighbourhood, losing what the current one has earned since. Card options spend the current neighbourhood's earnings first, then the earlier cash, so a walk whose card costs spent into what it would keep keeps what's left. The end screen shows what a walk lost as a Cash lost line
  - **Tips:** a tip is $1. Tips add to the pay, whatever the pack size, and they trade off against obstacles across a walk: the first neighbourhood spawns the most tips and the fewest obstacles, and each later one has fewer tips and more obstacles (31 tips and 72 plain obstacles down to 22 and 78), before each neighbourhood's own density scales them (from about two thirds of the plain obstacles in High Park to a quarter more in the Distillery District). Plain obstacles are fixed ones (benches, carts, cones, hedges, parked cars, trash cans and each neighbourhood's own) and movers, in the shares each neighbourhood's weights set. No stretch of more than 3 rows of road comes into view without a visible obstacle in it (a mover counts only once its body is on the road, not from its cue, and a squirrel never, as it blocks no lane), so the road never looks quiet for long, while busy bursts and calmer stretches still come at random.
  - **Pay:** the steady part. After each neighbourhood, each walking dog earns $20, the same in every neighbourhood. Pay is banked at the end of each neighbourhood: at its celebration, or on the end screen after neighbourhood 4. A firing loses that dog's pay for the current neighbourhood and the Walk bonus; its earlier pay stays banked
- **Owner happiness (♥):** one meter per dog, shown on its doghouse card; it never relies on colour alone. The dog's size sets its max (see Dogs); a smaller pack adds to its lead: walking 1 dog, its owner gets +2♥; walking 2, the front dog's owner gets +1♥; walking 3, nobody does. No owner's max goes above 6♥, whatever adds to it. While the pack chosen earns it, the start screen's Dogs band says so. Every heart looks the same, whatever adds it: the extra hearts a bonus adds (the small-pack bonus, Lucky collar) are drawn like the rest, and holding the card names each source. Every owner starts full, and treats restore hearts up to the max. At 0 the owner fires you with a quip and the dog leaves
- **Every change shows its cause:** each ♥ change shows its amount over the dog and on its doghouse card ("−1♥"), with its cause in the message strip, and each $ change a label with its amount alone ("+$1", "−$20"; see Heart changes and Money changes); a card's enlarged view lists the modifiers behind its numbers

### A walk

- A walk is 4 neighbourhoods of Toronto. Each neighbourhood is the same distance and takes about 2 minutes; a full walk takes about 8 minutes
- After neighbourhoods 1 to 3, the neighbourhood celebration, then a fork. Neighbourhood 4 has no fork
- **Forks:** the road meets a cross street; after neighbourhoods 1 and 2 a fork card offers three paths (left, straight, right), each leading to a neighbourhood you haven't walked this walk other than Yonge Street, sorted easiest on the left to hardest on the right. The fork after neighbourhood 3 leads only to Yonge Street, so it shows one path. Each path card shows the neighbourhood's name and difficulty, and the upgrade card it carries: the upgrade's name over a few words on what it does, said in full on the enlarged card. At a fork with more than one path, one path, drawn at random, carries no upgrade, and its card says "No upgrade" with no plaque; Yonge Street's path after neighbourhood 3 always carries one. The fork card's subtitle reads "Most paths bring an upgrade". Picking a path picks the next neighbourhood and its upgrade, if any, at once. A End walk button under the paths ends the walk there (see End walk)
- **Neighbourhoods** (6, each walk visits 4 with no repeats):

Each neighbourhood also has its own card obstacle (see Encounters and debuffs) and a few obstacles and movers found nowhere else; no mover or plain obstacle but the squirrel appears in more than 3 neighbourhoods, each street cart look counting as its own (see Moving hazards).

The street cart, a 2-lane obstacle that never moves, stands in every neighbourhood with that neighbourhood's look: flowers in Cabbagetown, fruit and veg in Kensington Market, hot dogs in High Park, Kensington Market and Yonge Street, ice cream in the Distillery District and The Beaches, and newspapers and magazines on Yonge Street. Where a neighbourhood has two looks, each cart picks one.

- The walk ends after neighbourhood 4, on End walk, or when no dogs are left. What it banks depends on how it ends (see Cash)
- **Interrupted walk:** the game saves the walk at each fork, as each neighbourhood starts, and whenever it's hidden. A walk closed before it ends resumes on the next launch at the start of the neighbourhood it was in, or at the fork it was waiting at, opening straight into the walk with the pause menu up; whatever happened in that neighbourhood before the close is undone and plays again. A saved walk that can't be resumed (it names a dog or neighbourhood the game no longer has, or the save is damaged) is banked as one that headed home
- **Walk bonus:** when every dog you set out with gets home at the end of neighbourhood 4, the walk pays a Walk bonus of $50 into the bank, whatever the pack size, shown on the end screen. Heading home early or a firing loses it
- **End screen:** the walk's total with a breakdown (pay, tips, lucky finds, Walk bonus, card costs, and in red the cash lost when the walk ended mid-neighbourhood), every row shown and a zero reading "$0" with no sign and the neighbourhoods walked, with no bank; a new-best ribbon when it beats the best walk with the same number of dogs; the badges the walk earned, each opening its badge card on a tap or a hold, in one row up to 7 and two rows past that; one closing line from each owner (a happier line from owners still walking, the fired line from owners who fired you)

### Choice cards

- Used for encounters and forks. Up to three across, art-led: an illustration plus a one-line effect; hold to enlarge for details
- Every card hits the whole pack. No card opens without a collision, except the fork card. A hit that opens a card costs nothing until you choose
- Each option's outcome is fixed and shown on the card, with hearts per dog ("−1♥ Ginger, −1♥ Kilo"); a card for three dogs reads "−1♥ each", and its enlarged view names each dog. A perk, gear item or upgrade that changes an option shows its charm centred just under that option's card: the item's icon on the left and, beside it, the item's name over what it did to that option (Hall pass's skip "Free skip", Quick recovery "Half time", Second chance "Safe", Charmer "Free"), with two charms stacked, the enlarged card included, never on the card, so the card keeps its full text box. The card row (title, cards and charms) rises to the 2/3 line of the walk view. Choices only, no minigames
- A money option shows its price on its plate, greyed out when you can't afford it, so cash never goes below $0; every card keeps at least one option that costs no money, and its plate is blank. Money options are charged once per card, whatever the pack size; heart options cost each owner, so a bigger pack has more owners who can fire you. Slower options cost money (missed tips) instead of time
- An option says what it would do before you pick: a debuff option states its stake ("Quick rinse → Stinky: tips pay nothing for 60s"), an option that would fire an owner says so ("Fires Jason"), and a heart Second chance would forgive reads as safe
- A fork's path card shows its upgrade's duration (Now, Next or All walk) where other cards show a price
- A card shows at most 3 options. Hall pass offers "Skip (Hall pass)" (a free option that skips the card) on every card until it's used in that neighbourhood; the skip replaces the option that costs the most hearts when a card already has 3 (the most expensive of those if they tie). Charmer and Hall pass can both apply to the same card: its money options are free and the skip is offered, and the player chooses; Charmer is used up by that card whichever option is picked
- After a card choice, each owner whose hearts changed reacts with a quip in the message strip

### Teaching, sound and settings

- **Back:** works the same in a browser tab and installed as an app. Each back closes the innermost thing open: a detail card (one held open during a walk closes as a tap closes it), Settings' install steps or the end screen's badge card, then the Pet shop, the Logbook, Help, Settings or a celebration. During a walk, back opens the pause menu; in the pause menu, it resumes the walk; on the Home question, it returns to the pause menu. On the end screen with nothing open, it goes home. On the home screen with nothing open, it asks "Quit?" with Keep playing (the default, key 1) and Quit (key 2); Quit leaves as the top bar's Exit does: an installed app closes, and a browser tab goes to the site's home page. Back or a tap outside keeps playing. Between a walk's end and the end screen, back does nothing. Among installed apps, only one the game can close asks Quit? and has Exit: Chrome's Android app, and Chrome's and Edge's app windows on a computer, opened straight into the game. In every other installed app (iPhone and iPad Home Screen apps, and Firefox's, Samsung Internet's and Edge's Android apps), back is the browser's own: it never asks Quit?, closes nothing in the game, and leaves it, and the home top bar has no Exit and Q does nothing. In a Firefox for Android tab, and in browsers without close watchers (older Chrome, and Safari), back before the first tap or key press after the page loads is the browser's own and leaves the game
- **First-time hints:** a one-line hint in the message strip the first time each thing appears (first puddle, first pull, first treat, the first moving obstacle's cue and so on); the help screen covers the rest. The firing hint jumps ahead of any waiting hint, so it shows before the first firing. Nothing teaches by overlaying the road: no tutorial overlays or swipe tips on the play area
- **Panels:** every page and overlay has one close control at the top right. Every modal card has one layout: its own background with no title bar, its title centred on its top row, and its close control at the right end of that row. Every modal card but the choice cards and the New walker celebration has that close control, and it does what a tap outside the card does: the pause menu's and the Home question's resume the walk, Quit?'s keeps playing, the purchase confirm's cancels, and the rest close. Tapping anywhere outside a panel closes it, the top bar's empty area, the walk bar's effect wells and the dog cards included (Help, the Logbook, the Pet shop, Settings, the badge celebration, the home screen's detail cards, the end screen's badge card and the Neighbourhood done celebration), and outside the pause menu or the Home question resumes the walk; only taps count, not drags. The top bar's buttons keep their own taps, a tap on a panel's empty space does nothing, and the tap that closes a panel does nothing else: it picks no dog and presses nothing under it. Choice cards and the New walker celebration wait for a button: a tap outside them does nothing. A card, a celebration or the end screen ignores taps for its first second, so a steering tap already on its way can't close it, pick an option or leave the end screen. A tap on the page beside the game (left or right of it on a wide screen) counts as outside too; the nav bar and the dev panel don't
- **Settings** (from pause and the home screen): sound on or off, music volume (45% on a new save), effects volume, reduced motion, and an Install row until the game is installed, which opens the browser's install prompt or, where the browser has none, shows its steps: on iOS Share, then Add to Home Screen, and in Firefox for Android the menu, then More, then Add app to Home screen. After the first finished walk, the walker's bubble on the next start screen suggests installing, once. When it runs installed, never in a browser tab, the game asks the browser to keep its saves
- **Sound:** every bump and pickup makes a sound. Short effects (tip, treat, bump, bark, card open, fired, a dodge, a gear find or swap, and the treat's sound again when a spare treat is given back), each lucky find's own sound, a sound for each card obstacle's hit (spray hiss, coin scatter, friendly woof), a splash on a puddle, a bark when a dog spots a squirrel and strains, and a sound of its own when the pull lands (a strain with no pull only barks), the heart-lost sound once after a card choice that costs any owner a heart once forgiveness is counted (a loss Second chance forgives plays nothing), a neighbourhood-done sound with Neighbourhood done (New walker keeps the badge chime), a badge-earned jingle and a new-best fanfare, and a music loop for each neighbourhood, which starts with its banner, cross-fading from the last over about a second, and plays on through its celebration and the fork. While the walk is paused by the player (the pause menu, Settings, Help or the Home question), the music drops to 30% volume and comes back on resume; it stays full under cards and the celebration, and goes silent while the tab is hidden. On iOS the game follows the silent switch: in silent mode it plays no sound

### Pickups

- **Tip:** +$1
- **Treat:** a grab item, not carried. It goes to the dog nearest where it touched (the front dog if only the walker touched it), whose owner gets +♥ up to the max; if that owner is already at max, the treat goes to the owner with the fewest hearts (ties to the dog nearer the front), and if every owner is at max, the Fanny pack keeps it when it has room; otherwise the treat pays $5 of cash, a gain like any other (see Money changes), counted with the tips on the end screen but never as a tip, so Stinky and Big tipper don't change it

### Dogs

A roster of 9, three in each size, each a fixed, named character with its own art and one fixed owner. You start with 1 dog slot and Archie, and buy up to 2 more slots and the other 8 dogs at the Pet shop. Dog prices mix sizes: each price band ($200 to $400, $600 to $800, $1,100 to $2,200) has dogs of at least two sizes, so a cheap dog can be large and a pricey one small. Walking more dogs is the main progression: more pay and a longer-lasting walk, but more owners who can fire you.

Dogs differ in play by size only:

| Size   | ♥   | Effects                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------ | --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Small  | 2   | Dodges 1 in 5 of the bumps that would land on it, with a "Dodge!" label; the hit costs nothing and doesn't count as a bump. Each small dog makes lane changes 10% faster; its detail card says both |
| Medium | 3   | The baseline                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Large  | 4   | Each large dog makes lane changes 10% slower, never below 10% slower than base; its detail card says so                                                                                             |

Size shows at a glance: on the road, medium dogs are drawn 48 px, large dogs 54 px and small dogs about 38 px, in every animation, with small dogs drawn chunky so they still read; doghouse faces and portraits differ less, about 1.2× and 0.8× on faces and 1.15× and 0.9× on portraits. Hit boxes don't change.

### Encounters and debuffs

Ten card obstacles open a choice card: the cheese crates, the other dog, the skunk and the thief, each in several neighbourhoods, and one of each neighbourhood's own, which appears only there (the wedding photo shoot also at The Beaches). No neighbourhood has two cards that bring the same debuff. A card offers the same options, with the same effects, in every neighbourhood it appears in. Each option costs one thing: money, hearts or a debuff. The one trade is the other dog's Let them sniff, money (missed tips) for hearts. Every money option costs $20. Most hearts options cost each walking dog's owner 1♥; the capybara's Help the keeper and the porch cat's Stand your ground cost only the lead dog's owner.

| Neighbourhood       | Card                | Options                                                                       |
| ------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| High Park           | Escaped capybara    | Help the keeper (−1♥ lead dog), Call the zoo ($20), Drag them away (−1♥ each) |
| Cabbagetown         | Porch cat           | Stand your ground (−1♥ lead dog), Treat bribe ($20), Bolt past (−1♥ each)     |
| Kensington Market   | Green bin spill     | Wade through (Muddy), Pay for cleanup ($20), Hurry past (−1♥ each)            |

| Yonge Street        | Big screen promo    | Wave hello (Spotlight), Buy the merch ($20), Push through (−1♥ each)          |

The cards found in several neighbourhoods:

| Card          | Neighbourhoods             | Options                                                                   |
| ------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cheese crates | Distillery, Kensington     | Have a sniff (Stinky), Buy the cheese ($20), Hurry on (−1♥ each)          |

The thief snatches the walker's tip pouch: Let the tips go loses it, Grab it back wins it back at a cost to the owners' patience, and Dogs give chase sends the pack after it. The escaped capybara is a mover: it steps once or twice, like any stepper. The wedding photo shoot is 2 lanes wide; the other neighbourhood cards are 1.

A puddle opens no card: hitting one costs no heart and makes the pack _Muddy_, with a plain obstacle's hit stop. Kensington Market, the Distillery and Yonge Street have potholes in its place: a water-filled pothole looks and sounds its own but is a puddle in every rule, Raincoats included.

Seven plain obstacles bring a debuff on contact: each is a puddle in every rule but its debuff, so the hit costs no heart and keeps a plain obstacle's hit stop. The frisbee dog (Cabbagetown) and the sign spinner (Yonge Street) bring Zoomies, trash cans (High Park, Cabbagetown), the seagull (The Beaches, Yonge Street) and barrel stacks (the Distillery) Stinky, and the stilt walker (Kensington Market, the Distillery) and the busker (Yonge Street) Spotlight. No neighbourhood has two contact sources of one debuff. Raincoats block only puddles and potholes.

Debuffs come from puddles, potholes, contact obstacles and card options. Cards add to what contact gives: Muddy from the dropped ice cream's Let them lick it (sticky paws) or the green bin's Wade through (slippery scraps); Stinky from the skunk's Quick rinse or the cheese crates' Have a sniff; Zoomies from the other dog's Pull away or the thief's Dogs give chase; Spotlight from the wedding shoot's Pose or the promo's Wave hello. Cabbagetown and High Park have no Spotlight; every other neighbourhood has all four. Debuffs are mild. Each lasts 60 seconds of walking (Zoomies 40), or until the neighbourhood ends, whichever comes first. Nothing on the road cures one; it runs out. Applying one the pack already has restarts its time; none stacks. Each is announced in the message strip when it starts and when it ends. While it lasts, a bar in the debuff's own colour runs the full width of the game just below the top bar, over the clouds, with the debuff's icon and name, and drains over its time; two active debuffs show as two bars. Hold a bar to see what the debuff does.

- **Muddy:** the pack's lane changes take about three times as long (67% slower). Bumps cost what they always do
- **Spotlight:** a bump costs $10 of cash on top of its hearts; with less on hand it takes what's left, and at $0 nothing. Second chance may forgive the heart, never the cash. A Spotlight bump keeps a bump's hit stop and grace window and counts toward bumps, bump-free neighbourhoods and bump badges
- **Stinky:** tips still appear, but a grabbed tip pays $0 and shows a red "+$0" label
- **Zoomies:** about once a second, the pack drifts one lane in the direction it last moved; at the road's edge it turns and drifts the other way. Steering still works, so you counter the drift. A drift never moves the pack into an obstacle it can't react to, the same as a pull

### Lucky finds

Good things to bump into on purpose. Each neighbourhood rolls its own number of each: on average 1 lost wallet and half a friendly neighbour, a quarter more at High Park and the Beaches, so some neighbourhoods have two of a kind and some none; two finds in one neighbourhood stand apart, in its first and last thirds. None opens a card: each bobs on the road and pays out on contact, with a short popup where it was grabbed and a quip from the owners who gained. The friendly neighbour walks about, one or two quick steps in any direction and with no cue, since a cue marks a hazard about to move, so the pack has to go and meet her; the lost wallet lies still.

- **Friendly neighbour:** pats for the pack, the hearts jackpot: 3♥ shared by the walking dogs, front dog first (a lone dog's owner +3♥; a pair's front dog +2♥ and the other +1♥; a trio +1♥ each), each owner capped at their max, so a smaller pack gains more per dog
- **Lost wallet:** +$$ found money, the money jackpot
- **Gear drop:** a gear item, about one a neighbourhood (on average 0.5, 0.8, 1.2 and 1.5 in neighbourhoods 1 to 4, two at most, never the same item twice in one), drawn among the gear not in effect, the cheaper more likely, in proportion to 1/price. Grabbing it puts it in effect for the rest of the walk on top of the Pocket item, owned or not; a later grab swaps it, and the earlier find goes back. Long reach doesn't reach it. A found item works from the moment it's grabbed: Hall pass from the current neighbourhood, the Fanny pack from the next treat it can keep, and Lucky collar raises owners' max ♥ (and lowers it again if swapped away, current hearts above it dropping to the max)

### Distractions

About 21 times a neighbourhood, a red herring: one of the neighbourhood's own people or animals, a kind that is a hazard there, most often steps onto an edge lane near the top of the road, lingers there a moment and steps back off; otherwise it is already standing in an edge lane and lingers before stepping off, or now and then waits at the road's edge and leaves. It is gone well before its row nears the pack. It never shows a mover's cue, so a hazard that cues is always real. Red herrings never touch anything, never stand on a pickup, an obstacle or a roadside prop, and leave the road's plan as it is.

### Moving hazards

Each obstacle has one look everywhere. Some kinds can move (each kind's movement is in the data), and every one that can always moves.

- **Cues:** a mover warns once: its first move is telegraphed about 0.6 seconds ahead, and its later moves come with no cue of their own, a stepper's and a hopper's each after a short random pause once the move before ends. A darting animal shows as soon as its stretch of road comes into view (at the road's top edge, if it sets off farther up), waiting at its edge until its cue. An in-lane mover's cue shows as soon as its stretch of road comes into view, so it can last longer than 0.6 seconds
- **Hits and pickups:** a mover may pass over a tip or a treat, which stays where it is, and may end a move on a tip, but never on a treat or a lucky find, so those can always be grabbed without a hit. A mover's hit is its standing obstacle's hit
- **Only ahead of the pack:** a mover moves only while it is still ahead of the pack: once it reaches the pack's rows it makes no more moves, and nothing ever moves back up the road toward a pack that has passed it
- **Placement:** every kind starts in any lane, more often in the lanes left clear longest, so the sides are never safe (see Spawning details)
- **Randomness:** every movement has plenty of randomness, since the less a mover can be predicted, the harder it is to dodge: each mover's speed varies by up to about 25% either way (a dart's by up to 40%, and where it sets off is drawn apart from its speed, so a slow or a fast one can come from high up or close), each step or hop takes a little more or less time than the last, and the pause before each step, hop or dart varies widely too, so no two movers of a kind move alike

Every mover uses one of twelve movement archetypes, shared across neighbourhoods, and each neighbourhood has 7 to 9 of them: Bounce, Cruise (in-lane), Dash and Swoop (darts; the ball dashes), Dive, Follow the leader, Hop, Merge, Stalk, Stop-and-go, Sway and Wander (steps). So the neighbourhoods stay distinct, no mover or plain obstacle appears in more than 3 of them; a card obstacle may, and so does the squirrel, which is in all 6.

- **Bounce:** a strict left-right hop, a lane to one side and then back, two to four hops, each after its own cue (the hopscotch kid, the sign spinner)
- **Darts:** cross the whole road (animals, such as the cat, the hen, the other dog and the squirrel). High Park's ferrets cross as a pair, 2 lanes wide. Each dart draws where it starts its crossing, from partway up the road to close to the pack, never so close that the pack has no time to react, so most crossings have to be dodged
  - **Angles:** the cat and the seagull always cross diagonally, coming down the road toward the pack, each at an angle it draws when it comes on, from a slight tilt to about 60 degrees, a steeper one starting farther up the road so it still leaves the pack time to react; in the third and fourth neighbourhoods of a walk, where the road goes by too fast for that, the steepest is about 50 and 40 degrees; the pigeons cross on a slight tilt up the road
  - **Turning back:** about a third of cats, ferrets, other dogs and seagulls turn back: somewhere from halfway to all the way across, drawn per dart, it turns with no cue of its own and crosses the whole road again the other way, keeping its tilt
  - **Stopping short:** some darting animals stop short instead, more or less often by how far the animal would really run: about half of cats (they freeze), a third of other dogs, and never a bird (pigeons, seagulls), a squirrel (so pulls stay simple) or the ball, which rolls straight across. One that stops does so with no cue: about three in four after 2, 3 or 4 lanes, drawn per dart, standing in that lane for the rest of its time on the road, and the rest on the far verge past the road's edge, out of play, where nothing can hit it. A dart that stops never turns back
- **Dive:** a dart that lands one to three lanes in from its edge, slides one lane further at a slower speed and stays down there; it never turns back (the frisbee dog, the volleyball player, which dives faster)
- **Follow the leader:** a group that steps a lane to either side as one block, each step after its leader's cue (the ducklings behind their mother, two or three steps; the tour group behind the guide's flag, one or two)
- **Hops:** quick hops at random to a lane on either side, a row up the road, or diagonally up, never down toward the pack, 1 or 2 spots at a time. The bunny hops two to five times. Shy hoppers (the bunny) sometimes shy away instead: about half of them, when the pack draws near, hop away from its side of the road (a lane farther from the pack, or up), so they clear the pack's lane rather than block it
- **In-lane:** keep their lane and move down the road at their own speed, faster than it scrolls (bikes, cars, the jogger, the Segway tour rider, the charging goose) or slower (the stroller, the garbage and ice cream trucks, the streetcar)
- **Merge:** an in-lane vehicle faster than the road that, partway down, signals and moves one lane over toward a side with room, then keeps going, always finishing before it reaches the pack (Yonge's taxi, and the delivery cyclist in Kensington and on Yonge)
- **Stalkers:** step toward the pack while still ahead of it: a lane to either side, a row down, or diagonally down, whichever brings it closest. The thief and the raccoon step two or three times, the skunk three to six and the coyote three or four, the first a little after coming on screen
- **Stop-and-go:** starts standing, then pushes down its lane slower than the road, stops for a beat and goes on, three or four pushes, each after its own cue (the gardener's wheelbarrow, the park ranger's cart, the brewery worker's keg dolly, the food cart and the ice cream bike)
  - **Crossers:** High Park's tourist train, the Distillery's brewery dray, The Beaches' patrol buggy (2 lanes wide) and Yonge Street's crosstown bus cross the road side-on, 3 lanes wide unless noted, slowly, each stopping once, the train for about a second and a half and the bus for about a second, the train ringing a bell and the bus opening its doors, before it goes on, always leaving the path's lane open. The train, the dray and the bus each stop covering the road's first three, middle three or last three lanes, so each may cover only part of the road, and the buggy on the second and third or the third and fourth lanes.
  - **Herring train:** about one in three tourist trains is a red herring instead: with no bell, it crosses the whole road far up the screen without stopping and is gone before its row nears the pack
- **Sway:** steps one lane over, pauses, and steps back, two or three times, rocking between two lanes; only its first step is cued (the couple, the stilt walker, the strolling busker)
- **Steps:** a few quick steps, each in any direction (a lane to either side, or a row up or down), with a short random pause and no cue between them (the granny cart shopper and the mail carrier three or four, the tourist two to four)

Each mover draws how many times it moves when it comes on, within its kind's range, so two of a kind can move different numbers of times. Every person and animal on the road moves, except the busker, the porch cat and the wedding photo shoot, which stand still. Vehicles (bikes, cars, scooters, the streetcar and trucks) only ever move up or down their own lane, never diagonally, and never sideways except the merging taxi and delivery cyclist. Benches, cones, hedges, parked cars, puddles and trash cans never move.

### Feedback

- **Bump flash:** every bump and puddle splash flashes a 4 px border in the bump's colour round the screen, with a 1 px ink keyline inside it, tints the road in that colour from 25% to nothing over the flash's 280 ms, and nudges the road 4 px for 150 ms; reduced motion keeps the border and tint and drops the nudge
- **Effect icons:** upgrades in effect, the walker's perk and the Pocket item show as small icons in the walk bar's wells. Hold one to see what it does. An effect that only makes more tips or treats appear (Keen eye, Rush hour) pops no icon on the road when one is grabbed, and neither does Long reach: a tip or treat it reaches shows a dotted line from the dog and a glint on its well, and its icon rides on the gain's chip, at the chip's left: a tip's "+$1" (a merged tip chip keeps it when any of its tips was reached), a treat's "+1♥", where it flies into the dog's card beside the heart, or the cash a treat pays when every owner is full
- **Firing moment:** when an owner fires you, their face pops up large just above the dog's doghouse card, never over it, with their fired line, a "FIRED!" plate shows, and the dog trots off the road edge. It plays right after the hit or card choice that fired you, and the walk holds still for about 3 seconds while it plays: the road, the pack and every mover stand still, and the face, plate, fired line and trot-off all end with the hold
- **Forgiven heart:** whenever Second chance forgives a heart, from a bump or a card choice, a label floats over the dog (like "Dodge!"), the heart on its doghouse card flashes back with a sparkle, and the message strip says so
- **Heart changes:** a lost heart pops over the dog on the road as a big broken heart with "−1♥", then arcs down into its doghouse card, where a label shows the amount alone ("−1♥"); the message strip names the cause. A gained heart pops in pink and flies up from the dog the same way. Every heart change plays this, whatever its cause
- **Hit moments:** every card obstacle's hit plays a big moment before its card: a burst over the pack, a brief flash at the screen edges, a large hit label over the pack, kept on screen, and the pack shaking off
- **Last heart:** when an owner drops to 1♥, a "{owner} has 1♥ left!" label rises over their doghouse card, and the card's rim pulses while they stay at 1♥
- **Message strip:** a strip over the clouds at the top of the backdrop band tells what just happened, one line at a time with its icon, on up to 2 rows. Each line shows for at least about 1.5 seconds before the next; a line still waiting after about 3 seconds is dropped, except hints and the firing line. It has a line for: a badge earned, a debuff starting and ending, a dodge, an effect starting (the effect line of an upgrade, the walker's perk or the Pocket item), a firing, first-time hints (see Teaching), a forgiven heart, the Walk bonus, a lucky find, an owner's quip (after a card choice or a lucky find), a bump, a pull's strain, a spare treat kept or spent, a treat, and a treat that pays cash. An owner's quip is styled like the other lines, led by the owner's face with their name under it in place of the icon, and its text has no name prefix
- **Money changes:** a money label shows only the amount, with no cause or modifier text
  - **Gains:** a gain's label, a tip's (a Stinky tip's red "+$0" included), a lucky find's, a treat's cash or a card option's, starts just by the cash total in the top bar and moves into it, so no gain's label covers the road; two at once stack one under the other. Tips grabbed within about a second of each other share one label that adds up ("+$2"); another gain never adds up with a tip
  - **Pay:** at a neighbourhood's end each dog's pay coin flies from its doghouse card to the cash, and one chip by the cash shows the neighbourhood's total pay, the Walk bonus included, until the last coin lands
  - **Losses:** a loss's label pops where the money changed hands, over the pack for a card option or a Spotlight bump, then flies up into the cash total, the way a heart flies into its doghouse card
- **Owner faces:** an owner at full hearts looks happy, whatever their max. Below full, an owner looks upset at 1♥, neutral at 2♥ or when more than one heart below their max, and happy otherwise (one below for an owner with more than 3♥)
- **Return home:** the end screen shows the walker and pack coming back up the front path while the owners wave; fired dogs are absent
- **Road markers:** things worth grabbing (tips, treats and lucky finds) bob gently; obstacles carry no marker. With reduced motion nothing bobs
- **Treat grabbed:** the owner smiles and the heart change plays (see Heart changes)

### Neighbourhood celebration

After each of neighbourhoods 1 to 3, before the fork, a short celebration plays over the paused road. After neighbourhood 4 the end screen takes its place, preceded by a New walker celebration for each walker finishing the walk unlocks.

- **Neighbourhood banner:** each neighbourhood opens with a banner over the road naming the world, the neighbourhood's number and its name; the player never sees the word "zone"
- **Neighbourhood done:** the walker you're walking with cheers, under a big title and the neighbourhood's name, with the pay it banks and its tips, leaving out a part that is $0. It waits for its Onward! button, a tap anywhere else, or key 1
- **New walker:** the first time you finish a walk that unlocks a walker (see Walkers), that walker runs in to join before the end screen, with their greeting, agility and perk, and waits for a tap on its button (key 1). When one walk unlocks several walkers, each joins in turn, in unlock order
- Confetti plays in both; with reduced motion it doesn't

### Upgrade cards

Walk-only boosts on the fork paths, from a pool of 6. Each fork draws its upgrades from the walk's seed, never from how the walk went: one path of a fork with more than one, at random, carries none, and every other path carries one drawn at random from the remaining pool, none twice and at most one trade-off. Picking a path takes its upgrade, which leaves the pool for the rest of the walk; "next neighbourhood" is the one that path leads to. Effects without "now" or "next neighbourhood" last the rest of the walk.

- Belly rubs: +1♥ to every owner now, up to their max
- Big tipper: every tip is worth $1 more in the next neighbourhood
- Good leash: no dog pulls toward squirrels
- Raincoats: puddles and potholes don't make the pack Muddy: no Muddy and no hit stop. A card's Muddy still applies
- Rush hour (trade-off): twice the tips and treats and more obstacles in the next neighbourhood
- Charmer: the first card in each neighbourhood has free money options (hearts and debuffs still cost; see Choice cards)

### Walkers

Four walkers. You start with Casey; each other walker unlocks the first time you finish a walk with at least so many dogs still walking at the end (see New walker): Mr. B 1, Ada 2 and Trista 3, so a walk finished with 3 dogs unlocks all three. Every walker walks the same road at the same speed, and a walker's effects stack with the Pocket item and upgrade cards.

**Agility** sets how fast the pack changes lanes, as a multiple of the base lane-change speed. Walkers spread from 1.0 to 1.4, Running shoes add 1.0, each small dog in the pack adds 0.1 and each large dog takes 0.1 off, never below 0.9. Players never see the word agility, and see it only as a percentage against the base: walker cards say "+40% lane change speed" (Ada) or "+10% lane change speed" (Casey), and a walker at the default speed (Mr. B) shows no lane-change line at all, Running shoes "+100% lane change speed", a small dog "+10% lane change speed" and a large dog "−10% lane change speed", in the same words, amount first. The slowest pack's lane move (the 0.9 floor) sets the row spacing.

Each walker has one **perk**:

- **Keen eye** (Mr. B): 50% more tips and treats appear
- **Long reach** (Trista): the front dog grabs tips and treats one lane to either side
- **Quick recovery** (Ada): debuffs last half as long
- **Second chance** (Casey): the first ♥ lost in each neighbourhood is forgiven (one heart; never Spotlight's cash)

Everything bought at the Pet shop is permanent; upgrade cards are the walk-only boosts. Progress (bank, owned items, stats, achievements) is saved between sessions; storage is in [`src/README.md`](../src/README.md). A save from a newer version of the game is left untouched for the session, and the home screen shows the update notice's plate under the top bar reading "Newer save", with an "Update now" button that restarts the game on the newer version; when an update is also waiting, the update notice shows instead.

- **Layout, top to bottom:** the top bar (the bank centred, with Logbook, Help, Sound, Settings and Exit on the right); the gate scene; then three framed sections in order, headed Pick a walker, Pick your dogs and Pick your gear (the Pocket, optional); then the Start walk button at the bottom. - **Update notice:** when a new version of the game is waiting, the home screen shows a plate under the top bar, over the gate scene, reading "Update available" with a green "Update now" button that loads the new version. It shows only on the home screen, never during a walk, and stays until tapped

On a brand-new save, the starter's owner waits at the gate until the first walk, and Casey and Archie start picked, so the first walk is one tap on Start walk

  - **Dogs tab:** lists all 9 dogs, cheapest first, each row showing its size and hearts and marking owned ones as Gear does, and all 3 dog slots in order, the starting one included, owned ones marked as for dogs and gear (each one holds 1 dog; the first is free, the second costs $1,000 and the third $3,000; slots can be bought in any order, and the pack holds one dog per slot owned; one for sale looks like a dog or gear item for sale)
  - **Gear tab:** lists the 5 items, each with a short effect line, marking owned ones
  - **Headings and buying:** the Gear, Dogs and Dog slots headings each show how many you own of all there are ("1 of 9 owned"). Tap an item to buy it, at any time you can pay, then confirm in a box that names it and shows the bank left after (Buy or Cancel). Rows for sale follow the For sale tile state. On the home screen the Pet shop building gets a gold outline while anything in it is affordable. Tapping a dog or dog slot for sale on the home screen opens the shop at that item
- **Dog slot sign:** a small sign hanging from the Pet shop's awning, across its front, reading "Walk more dogs!" over a dog slot's price. It shows while a dog slot is for sale, gets the Pet shop's gold outline while the bank covers that slot, and a tap opens the shop at the lowest-numbered slot for sale
- **Tile states** (walkers, dogs and Pocket gear alike, each state plainly distinct from the others at a glance, and the same in all three rows): every tile shows its real picture, never a silhouette. For sale (not owned, on the start screen and in the Pet shop alike): a grey tile or row; when the bank covers it, its picture shows in full colour with a green dollar bill and a green buy plate with the price, and when it doesn't, the picture is greyed with a closed padlock and a grey plate with the price in red; Locked walker: the picture greyed, with a closed padlock and its goal plate; Available (owned, not picked): full colour on a cream tile in a plain frame; Picked: full colour in a gold frame with a check; Pack full (owned, not picked, while every slot is filled): greyed in colour, and a tap says the pack is full

- **Start button:** the only button below the sections, reading "Start walk" in every state, and not ready until a walker and a dog are picked (the Pocket is optional). Each dog tile shows its pay
- **Walker picker:** choose from the walkers you've unlocked. Walkers, dogs and the Pocket item share one pick toggle: tap to pick, tap the picked one again to unpick it. The walker choice is saved and comes back picked, unless it was unpicked. Every walker tile, owned or locked, carries the walker's name on a grey plate; locked walkers use the Locked walker tile state; tapping one shakes it and the bubble gives the goal
- **Walker's bubble:** every home screen message shows in the walker's speech bubble (lengths in Voice). - **Gear and Pocket** (5 items, bought once at the Pet shop, priced by how much each helps: Fanny pack, then Hall pass, then Running shoes, then Lucky collar, then Whistle):
  - **Fanny pack:** keeps one spare treat: a treat grabbed while every walking owner is at their max goes into the pack instead of paying its cash, and the moment any owner loses a heart, the spare treat gives it back, so an owner on their last heart doesn't fire you; when several lose one at once, it goes to the owner left with the fewest hearts, ties to the front dog; Second chance's forgiveness comes first. Its walk bar well shows whether a treat is kept, and a spent treat flies from the well to the dog's doghouse card
  - **Hall pass:** once per neighbourhood, skip one encounter card for free; see Choice cards
  - **Lucky collar:** every owner has +1 max ♥
  - **Running shoes:** lane changes +100%
  - **Whistle:** medium and large dogs also dodge 1 in 5 of the bumps that would land on them, as small dogs do
  - **Pocket:** put 1 owned item in your Pocket before each walk, or none. The Pocket section is a strip of all 5 items: tap an owned item to pocket it (gold frame), tap it again to empty the Pocket; an unowned item is For sale with its price, and tapping it opens the shop on it. The section's band names the pocketed item, or says one per walk when the Pocket is empty; hold an item for its effect. The item is kept, never used up, and switches freely between walks; its effects refresh each walk
- **Logbook** (the top bar's Logbook button opens it directly on the Badges tab, with no card in between; the screens say "walk", never "run"): the Walks tab lists walks started; walks finished, headed home or out of dogs, under How walks ended; dogs walked, across all walks; the most earned in one walk, with that walk's neighbourhoods and dogs; lifetime earnings; total firings and the owner who fired you most; most neighbourhoods finished in one walk (heading home mid-neighbourhood doesn't count it). Every tab is one column on the same cream panel in the same text size, and scrolls when it runs long. No heading repeats the open tab's name. The Dogs tab lists one block per dog: its face and name, then its walks, neighbourhoods finished and times fired, one per row
- **Achievements** (the badges are in [`src/data/achievements.ts`](../src/data/achievements.ts), in four groups: firsts, mastery, collection, silly): the Badges tab of the Logbook, one badge per row with its name, goal and progress, earned badges in colour on a gold band with a tick, and locked ones greyed; a locked badge that counts across walks also shows its progress ("7/75"). Badges come in about equal thirds for a typical player: easy ones within a few walks, medium ones within 10 to 30, and hard ones taking 40 or more, so the full set is still the last thing a player completes, after buying out the Pet shop. A badge that counts bumps counts every bump that lands, forgiven ones included, never a dodged one, the same as the bump-free badges. Each badge shows its name, goal and one line. Earning one plays a short celebration (owners cheer) wherever it happens; there is no other reward. A money goal counts the walk's total on the end screen, checked when it's banked. A dodge, by any dog, doesn't count as a bump for the bump-free badges. A streak badge counts walks in a row; a firing ends the streak, and heading home early neither ends it nor adds to it. A badge for a card option counts each time that option is picked, across walks

### Spawning details

How the planner places the road so it keeps the Spawning and Moving hazards rules.

- **Counts:** each neighbourhood's obstacle, tip and card counts are set to about what its road can hold, and the planner keeps each kind near its weight's share of what appears, wide ones included, so the road matches the numbers it is tuned by
- **Gaps:** wherever the path changes lanes between two obstacles, the road between their hit boxes, as drawn, is at least the tallest pack's height plus the one or two lane changes it makes there at a Muddy pack's speed, about a third of the slowest pack's, plus about half a second to react. Off the path, obstacles may stand closer. Two obstacles 3 lanes wide never sit in neighbouring rows: at least one empty row separates them. Narrower ones may, wherever the gap rule and the 2 open lanes per row still hold
- **Mover limits:** at most ten movers are on screen at once (at most six darting and four in-lane movers slower than the road); squirrels and the friendly neighbour block no lane and count only among themselves, against the same limits
- **Mover placement:**
  - **Crossers:** each crosser draws its stop evenly among its stops and is placed where that stop keeps the path open; where none fits, it tries its other stops in turn, in a random order
  - **Darts:** a dart may set off from a spot something else already holds, crossing over or past it, so a fixed obstacle may stand, and an in-lane mover may pass, where only a dart will cross. A dart that fits no row from where it was drawn to set off tries again from farther up the road, as far as its cue still shows, then at another angle, then at another speed from its kind's range, the nearest to its own first, so a dart is skipped only when none of those fits. A dart that blocks a lane (any dart but a squirrel) never blocks the path while it meets the pack, and obstacles may stand on the row before its crossing like any other
  - **Lanes:** two in-lane movers may share a lane when they never meet, always at least a cell's length apart
  - **No room:** a mover that doesn't fit where it was planned comes on at the nearest later spot that fits. A mover with no room on screen waits to come on; a card obstacle with no room stands still in its place instead, and one that doesn't fit its row goes to the nearest row that does, so no card is ever skipped
- **Path:** each neighbourhood draws its path first: it holds each lane for at least 2 rows, its lane stays open in every row, and every lane it crosses to change lanes stays clear in the row before the change. It carries on from the last neighbourhood's lane, and a walk starts it in the pack's lane. Wherever lane-blocking spawns wall off a stretch of road in a row, that stretch keeps a lane no dart sweeps, so a pack that dodged into it is never trapped
- **Quiet stretches:** a quiet stretch at a neighbourhood's first or last row runs half as long at most, so two neighbourhoods' quiet ends never add up, and a longer quiet stretch gets one of the neighbourhood's own fixed obstacles moved into it, except where no fixed obstacle can fit (a mover's path, the drawn path, or the open lanes and reaction gaps the rules keep)
- **Swaps:** a fixed obstacle that fits nowhere is swapped for one of the neighbourhood's narrower fixed obstacles, 2 lanes and then 1, drawn by their weights, so the road keeps its count. Card obstacles and the puddle are never swapped
