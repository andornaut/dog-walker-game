Roadside

Woof Way's lawns beyond the sidewalks carry OutRun-style roadside billboards: front-facing houses behind picket fences or hedges, mailboxes, street trees, lamp posts and yard clutter (a gnome, a kid's bike, flower beds, a sprinkler, a washing line, the plum cat on a fence). Today those lawns are bare stripes, so the street reads as a road through a field; the houses make it the suburb the backdrop shows at the T-junction.

Every prop's inner edge is at least 192 road px from the centre line (the sidewalk ends at 188), so on screen no prop crosses a sidewalk or a lane. Props scale with depth from the far edge (0.5) toward the pack and leave the screen's sides at about 0.9. They draw under the backdrop band, rising from behind its bottom edge as the road's objects do, so the T-junction, its approach rows and the gag spots are unchanged. The top bar, backdrop, doghouses, road objects and pack are today's.

Placement: a slot every 112 road px on each side, lots and gaps alternating, the right side one slot behind the left, so a house on one side faces a tree, lamp post or washing line on the other, and each side passes a house every 224 px (1.9 s at 120 px/s). Six lots and five gaps cycle with a different stride per side. About 21 props are in view at once.

What the game would need: these sprites with their @88, @75, @62 and @50 variants; a per-zone roadside table (lots, gaps, slot spacing and across offsets, as above); and from tech, code that spawns the props from that table as the road scrolls, projects each at its across and depth step with the road camera, draws them back to front with the road objects (under the backdrop band and the doghouses, a ground shadow under the free-standing ones) and drops them once they leave the screen's sides.
